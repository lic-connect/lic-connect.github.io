Insurance Surrender Terms

Surrender Value: The Surrender Value is the cash amount offered by an insurance company to the policyholder upon voluntarily terminating the policy before its maturity or the insured event occurs. This value is typically acquired only after premiums have been paid for a minimum period (usually 2 or 3 full years). Once a policy is surrendered, the life cover ceases and the contract ends. It represents the "savings" portion of your insurance premium after deducting administrative expenses and protection costs.

1. Paid-Up Capital (Paid-Up Value)

If a policyholder stops paying premiums after the policy has acquired a surrender value but does not officially surrender it, the policy becomes "Paid-Up." The original Sum Assured is reduced in proportion to the number of premiums paid.

Paid-Up Value = (Sum Assured) × (No. of Premiums Paid / No. of Premiums Payable)

2. Guaranteed Surrender Value (GSV)

This is the minimum amount guaranteed by the insurer to the policyholder. It is calculated by taking a specific percentage of the total premiums paid (excluding the first year premium and any rider/extra premiums) plus the surrender value of any vested bonuses.

GSV = (Total Premiums Paid × GSV Factor) + (Vested Bonuses × Bonus Factor)

3. Special Surrender Value (SSV)

The SSV is usually higher than the GSV. It depends on the duration for which the premiums are paid and the total "Paid-Up Value" accrued. It is calculated using the company's current financial performance and actuarial expectations. LIC usually pays the higher of the GSV or SSV to the policyholder.

SSV = (Paid-Up Value + Vested Bonus) × SSV Factor

Note: Factors for GSV and SSV vary based on the plan type, the entry age of the policyholder, and the duration of the policy at the time of surrender.
