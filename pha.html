1. Lapsed Policy: The Danger Zone

What is a Lapsed Policy?

A lapsed policy is a life insurance policy in which the due premium has not been paid within the grace period, specifically before completion of the minimum qualifying premiums (usually 2 full years).

  • The policy becomes Inactive.
  • Life cover stops immediately.
  • Benefits are suspended and not payable.

When exactly does a policy lapse?

A policy lapses immediately after the grace period expires if the premium remains unpaid. The lapse date is the day after the grace period ends.

Mode of Premium Grace Period
Yearly / Half-yearly / Quarterly 30 Days
Monthly 15 Days

Is a lapsed policy active?

❌ NO. A lapsed policy is NOT active. It exists only as a "revivable contract".

  • ❌ Death Benefit: Not Payable (Claim = ₹0).
  • ❌ Maturity Benefit: Not Payable.
  • ❌ Bonus: Stops accruing.
  • ❌ Loan Facility: Not available.

Detailed Mathematical Example

Policy Details (Assumed):
DOC: 01-01-2022 | Term: 20 Yrs | SA: ₹5,00,000
Annual Premium: ₹25,000 | Min premiums required: 2 Years
Payment History:
Year 1 (01-01-2022): ✅ Paid
Year 2 (Due 01-01-2023): ❌ Not Paid

Timeline of Lapse:
Grace Period Ends: 31-01-2023 (30 days from due date)
Policy Status on 01-02-2023: LAPSED

Consequence:
If death occurs on 10-03-2023:
Claim Payable = ₹0 (Total Loss of Cover)

Revival: The Way Back

A lapsed policy can be revived within 5 years from the First Unpaid Premium (FUP).

Requirements: Pay all unpaid premiums + Interest on arrears + Satisfy health requirements (if asked).

Revival Cost (Illustrative):
Unpaid Premiums: ₹50,000
Interest (approx 9.5%): ₹4,750
Total to Pay: ₹54,750 -> Policy becomes In-Force.

What if NOT revived?

If not revived within 5 years, the policy becomes Foreclosed / Terminated. Revival is permanently closed, and only Surrender Value (if any) may be paid.

LIC-Ready Summary: A lapsed policy is an inactive policy where premiums are unpaid beyond grace period (before 2 years), resulting in loss of ALL benefits. It can be revived within 5 years.

2. Paid-Up Policy: The Safety Exit

What is a Paid-Up Policy?

A Paid-Up policy is a status where the policyholder stops paying premiums after paying for a minimum qualifying period. Unlike a lapsed policy, the policy does NOT lapse. Instead, it continues as an active policy with reduced benefits.

The Minimum Premium Rule

For most LIC traditional plans, the Golden Rule is:

Minimum Premiums Paid must be ≥ 2 Full Years

If you stop paying before 2 years, the policy Lapses (Total Loss).
If you stop paying after 2 years, the policy becomes Paid-Up (Partial Value Saved).

Is a Paid-Up Policy Active?

✅ YES. It remains active till maturity. However:

  • Sum Assured: Reduces proportionately.
  • Bonus: Stops accruing (future bonuses are lost).
  • Risk Cover: Continues but at a reduced value.

Detailed Mathematical Example

Policy Details:
Plan: Endowment | Term: 20 Yrs | SA: ₹5,00,000
Annual Premium: ₹25,000
Premiums Paid: 2 Years (2020, 2021)
Unpaid Premium: 3rd Year (2022)
1. Formula for Paid-Up Sum Assured (PU-SA):
PU-SA = (No. of Premiums Paid ÷ Total Payable) × Original SA
= (2 ÷ 20) × 5,00,000
= 0.10 × 5,00,000
= ₹50,000 (New Reduced SA)

2. Benefits Payable:
On Death or Maturity: ₹50,000 (PU-SA) + Bonus accrued during first 2 years only.

Loss: Full SA of ₹5L is lost. Future bonuses for 18 years are lost.

Can a Paid-Up Policy be Revived?

❌ NO. (In the context of typical policy health advice). Once a policy acquires Paid-Up status and the revival period expires, the contract is modified permanently. You cannot simply start paying again to restore the original 5 Lakhs cover later.

Paid-Up vs. Lapsed (The Verdict)

Factor Paid-Up Lapsed
Status ✅ Active ❌ Inactive
Death Benefit Reduced Nil (Zero)
Maturity Reduced Nil (Zero)
Verdict Safe Harbor Danger Zone

Is Paid-Up a good strategy?

Sometimes Yes. It is a legitimate option if you are in financial stress, or if you feel the premium is too high but don't want to lose the money already paid. It is a "Safety Exit".

LIC Advisory: Paid-Up is a safety exit, not a growth strategy. It preserves your money but freezes your growth. Always better than a Lapse, but worse than Continuing.

3. Foreclosure: The Point of No Return

What is Foreclosure?

Foreclosure is the permanent termination of a life insurance policy by LIC. Unlike a lapse (which can be fixed), foreclosure is final. It happens when the contract ends permanently, no revival is allowed, and no insurance benefit remains.

When does Foreclosure happen?

It occurs in two specific situations:

  1. Non-Revival: A lapsed policy is not revived within the allowed revival period (5 years).
  2. Loan Exhaustion: When the outstanding Loan + Interest exceeds the Surrender Value.

Mathematical Example 1: Non-Revival (Time Expiry)

Policy Details:
DOC: 01-01-2015 | Term: 20 Yrs
Paid Premium: Only 1 Year (2015)
Lapsed Date: 01-02-2016 (After grace period)

The Clock Ticks:
Revival Window (5 Yrs): Ends on 31-12-2020.
Policyholder Action: None.

Result on 01-01-2021:
Status: FORECLOSED
Death Claim: ₹0
Maturity: ₹0
Revival: ❌ Permanently Closed.

Mathematical Example 2: Loan Default (The Debt Trap)

This is dangerous because it happens silently. If you take a loan and don't pay interest, the debt grows while your policy value (Surrender Value) grows slowly.

Scenario:
Policy Status: Paid-Up
Surrender Value (SV): ₹1,20,000

Loan Details:
Loan Taken: ₹90,000
Interest Rate: 9% p.a.
Time Passed: 5 Years (No interest paid)

Calculation:
Outstanding Loan with Interest ≈ ₹1,32,000

The Trigger Point:
Debt (₹1.32L) > Asset Value (₹1.20L)

Result:
LIC adjusts the SV against the loan.
Balance Payable to You: ₹0
Policy Status: FORECLOSED immediately.

Comparison: Lapsed vs. Foreclosed

Feature Lapsed Foreclosed
Status Inactive Terminated
Revival ✅ Allowed (within 5 yrs) ❌ Not Allowed
Hope Can be restored End of the road

Important LIC Insight

"Foreclosure is not a choice — it is a penalty."
Summary: Foreclosure is the permanent termination of a policy due to non-revival or loan exhaustion, resulting in total loss of benefits and no scope for revival.

4. Policy Loan: Liquidity with Responsibility

What is a Policy Loan?

A policy loan is an advance given by LIC against the Surrender Value (SV) of your own policy. It is not an external loan; it is your own money unlocked early without closing the policy.

Eligibility & Limits

  • Condition: Policy must have acquired Surrender Value (usually after 2 full years).
  • Status: Available for In-Force and Paid-Up policies. (NOT for Lapsed policies).
Maximum Loan Amount:
  • In-Force Policy: Up to 90% of Surrender Value.
  • Paid-Up Policy: Up to 85-90% of Surrender Value.
Example:
Surrender Value (SV): ₹1,50,000
Max Loan = 90% of 1,50,000 = ₹1,35,000

Loan Interest Mechanics

Interest is charged Half-Yearly based on a rate declared by LIC (approx 9% - 10% p.a.). It is Simple Interest, not compound, provided you pay it regularly.

Interest Calculation Example:
Loan Amount: ₹1,00,000
Interest Rate: 9% p.a.
Duration: 3 Years (No repayment made)

Interest = 1,00,000 × 9% × 3 = ₹27,000
Total Outstanding = ₹1,27,000

Policy Loan vs. Bank Loan

Feature Policy Loan Bank Loan
Credit Score ❌ Not Required ✅ CIBIL Mandatory
Processing Fast (Same day/24 hrs) Slow (Documentation)
EMI ❌ Not Mandatory ✅ Mandatory
Default Risk Policy Foreclosure Legal Action / Agents

The Hidden Trap: Effect on Maturity

While a loan does not stop your Bonus from accruing, it reduces your final payout rupee-for-rupee.

Maturity Calculation with Loan:
Gross Maturity (SA + Bonus): ₹8,00,000
Less: Outstanding Loan Principal: (-) ₹1,00,000
Less: Unpaid Interest: (-) ₹50,000
Net Maturity Paid: ₹6,50,000

Crucial Warning: The Foreclosure Trigger

If you do not pay interest, it is added to the principal. If (Loan + Interest) > Surrender Value, the policy is immediately FORECLOSED (Terminated) and you get nothing.

Verdict: A policy loan is a secured advance carrying moderate interest. While safer than bank loans, failure to manage interest can lead to foreclosure and loss of benefits.

5. Surrender Value: The Scrap Value

What is Surrender Value?

Surrender Value (SV) is the amount payable by LIC when a policyholder voluntarily terminates the policy before maturity. It is the cash value of your exit.

  • ❌ Policy ends permanently.
  • ❌ No revival possible.
  • ❌ No future insurance cover.

When can you Surrender?

For most traditional plans, the policy acquires a surrender value only after payment of at least 2 Full Years' Premiums. Before this, the value is usually ₹0.

The Two Types of SV (GSV vs SSV)

LIC calculates two values and pays you the HIGHER of the two:

  1. GSV (Guaranteed Surrender Value): A fixed percentage defined in the policy bond (e.g., 30% of premiums paid excluding 1st year).
  2. SSV (Special Surrender Value): A non-guaranteed value based on the LIC's current financial performance and market factors. (Usually, SSV is higher).

Detailed Mathematical Example

Policy Scenario:
Sum Assured: ₹5,00,000 | Annual Premium: ₹25,000
Paid: 6 Years (Total ₹1,50,000)
Bonus Accrued: ₹1,12,500
Step 1: Calculate Paid-Up SA
(6 / 20) × 5,00,000 = ₹1,50,000

Step 2: Calculate SSV Base
Base = Paid-Up SA + Accrued Bonus
Base = 1,50,000 + 1,12,500 = ₹2,62,500

Step 3: Apply SSV Factor
Assume Factor based on duration is 0.55 (55%)
SV = 2,62,500 × 0.55 = ₹1,44,375

Result: You paid ₹1.50 Lakhs, you get back ₹1.44 Lakhs. (Loss of Principal + Interest + Cover).

Rule of Thumb: What % do you get back?

Policy Duration Approx SV (% of Premiums Paid) Verdict
1 - 3 Years 0% - 30% ❌ Huge Loss
4 - 7 Years 35% - 50% ⚠ Significant Loss
8 - 12 Years 55% - 70% ⚠ Moderate Loss
Near Maturity 80% - 95% ✔ Acceptable (Rarely)

Surrender vs. Paid-Up Decision

Situation Better Option
Financial Emergency Loan (Keep policy active)
Cannot Pay Premium Paid-Up (Keep reduced cover)
Policy totally useless Surrender (Exit & reinvest)

Advisory

"Surrender Value is the 'Scrap Value' of your policy. Early surrender destroys your capital."
Summary: Surrender Value is the amount payable on voluntary termination. It is calculated based on premiums paid and bonuses, but usually results in a financial loss if done in early years.

6. Paid-Up vs Loan vs Surrender: The Decision Matrix

This is the most critical decision a policyholder faces when they cannot pay premiums. Which option minimizes loss and maximizes value?

1. The Golden Rule (Memorize This)

🥇 LOAN is Best   >   🥈 PAID-UP is Next   >   🥉 SURRENDER is Last

2. The Scenario (For Fair Comparison)

Let's compare all 3 options on the same policy at the same time.

Policy Details:
Plan: Endowment (20 Years)
Sum Assured (SA): ₹5,00,000
Annual Premium: ₹25,000
Premiums Paid: 6 Years (Total Paid: ₹1,50,000)
Bonus Accrued (6 yrs): ₹1,12,500
Surrender Value (Approx): ₹1,44,375

Option 1: Take a LOAN (The Winner)

You take a loan against the policy to solve your immediate cash crunch. You continue the policy.

Math:
Loan Taken: ₹1,00,000 (Available limit)
Interest (Calculated for 10 remaining yrs @ 9%): ₹90,000
Total Debt at Maturity: ₹1,90,000

Original Maturity Value (SA+Bonus): ₹8,00,000
Net Payout to You: ₹8,00,000 - ₹1,90,000 = ₹6,10,000
  • ✅ Pros: Immediate cash (₹1L), Policy stays FULLY active, Death cover remains Full (₹5L+).
  • ✅ Verdict: Highest total value delivered.

Option 2: Make it PAID-UP (The Safe Harbor)

You stop paying premiums. You take NO money out now. The policy shrinks but stays alive.

Math:
Paid-Up SA Formula: (No. of Premiums Paid ÷ Total Term) × SA
= (6 ÷ 20) × 5,00,000 = ₹1,50,000

Maturity Value: Paid-Up SA + Accrued Bonus
= 1,50,000 + 1,12,500 = ₹2,62,500
  • ✅ Pros: Zero risk of foreclosure, Policy continues till end.
  • ❌ Cons: No immediate cash, future bonuses stop accumulating.

Option 3: SURRENDER (The Last Resort)

You close the policy immediately and take whatever LIC gives you.

Math:
Surrender Value (SV) ≈ ₹1,44,375
Future Value: ₹0
  • ❌ Cons: Immediate termination. No death benefit. Massive financial loss compared to premiums paid (₹1.5L) and future potential.

3. Side-by-Side Comparison

Option Immediate Cash Future Value Policy Status
LOAN ₹1,00,000 ₹6,10,000 ✅ ALIVE (Full)
PAID-UP ₹0 ₹2,62,500 ✅ ALIVE (Reduced)
SURRENDER ₹1,44,375 ₹0 ❌ DEAD

4. When to choose what? (Time-Frame Logic)

  • Early Years (1-3 yrs): ❌ Never Surrender (You get nearly nothing). ✔ Take Loan if available.
  • Mid Years (4-10 yrs): ✔ Loan is Best. ✔ Paid-Up is safe if income has stopped permanently. ❌ Surrender only for dire emergencies.
  • Near Maturity (Last 3-4 yrs): ✔ Loan preferred. ⚠ Surrender is acceptable only if the SV is very close to Maturity value (rare).
Final Verdict: Loan is temporary pain; Surrender is permanent damage. Always calculate the "Paid-Up Maturity" before deciding to surrender.

Riders Explained: Enhance Your Policy

Riders are optional add-ons to enhance a basic life insurance policy. Below is a detailed breakdown of the 5 key riders, their importance, and mathematical impact.

1. ADDb – Accidental Death and Disability Benefit

What is it? Provides an extra sum assured if death occurs due to an accident. It also pays benefits in case of permanent disability due to an accident.

Importance: High for people with risky jobs (drivers, construction) or high commute.

Mathematical Example:
Basic Sum Assured (SA): ₹10,00,000
ADDb Rider SA: ₹5,00,000
If Death in Accident:
Total Payout = ₹10L (Base) + ₹5L (Rider) = ₹15,00,000

2. Accident Benefit Rider

What is it? Covers death or permanent total disability due to accident. Unlike ADDb which focuses heavily on the lump sum death part, this often focuses on disability income protection.

Example:
If permanent disability occurs:
A monthly income (e.g., ₹25,000) may be paid for a set term (e.g., 10 years) to replace lost income.

3. Term Assurance Rider

What is it? Provides extra life cover on top of your basic plan for a limited term.

Importance: High for breadwinners who need higher protection but can't afford a huge basic Endowment SA.

Mathematical Example:
Basic SA: ₹10,00,000
Term Rider SA: ₹15,00,000
If Death occurs:
Total Payout = ₹10L + ₹15L = ₹25,00,000

4. Premium Waiver Benefit (PWB)

What is it? Waives future premiums if the proposer suffers disability or death (crucial for minor lives).

Example: Annual Premium is ₹50,000. After 5 years, proposer dies. LIC waives the remaining 15 years of premium. The policy continues, and the child gets the full Maturity Benefit later.

5. Critical Illness (CI) Rider – Deep Dive

What is it? Pays a lump sum if diagnosed with a critical illness (Cancer, Heart Attack, Kidney Failure, etc.). The payout is additional to life cover.

Comparison: LIC CI Rider vs. Standalone Private Policy

Feature LIC CI Rider Standalone Health/CI Policy
Premium Low (Budget Friendly) Higher
Coverage Moderate (₹10L–₹50L) Very High (₹50L–₹5Cr)
Illnesses Limited (10–15) Broader (20–50+)
Payout Lump Sum Only Lump Sum OR Hospital Bill Reimbursement
Verdict Good for budget/basic cover Best for high medical protection
Mathematical Scenario (35 Years Old):
Case A: LIC CI Rider
- Diagnosed with Stage 1 Cancer.
- Payout: ₹5,00,000 Lump Sum.
- Base policy continues for death/maturity.

Case B: Standalone Policy
- Payout: ₹20,00,000 (or actual hospital bill).
- Covers actual high-cost treatment.

How to Choose? (Priority Order)

  1. Critical Illness Rider: (Most Important) Medical costs are the biggest threat to savings.
  2. Term Assurance Rider: If your family is financially dependent on you.
  3. ADDb Rider: If you commute daily or have a risky job.
  4. Premium Waiver: Mandatory for Child Plans.

Final Impact Analysis

Scenario Total Death Benefit Extra Benefit
Base Policy Only ₹10,00,000 None
+ Term Rider (₹15L) ₹25,00,000 High Cover
+ ADDb (₹5L) ₹15,00,000 (if Accidental) Disability Cover
+ CI Rider (₹5L) ₹10,00,000 ₹5L Immediate Payout on Illness
Conclusion: If budget is tight, at least take the Critical Illness Rider. If you want full family protection, take Term Rider + CI + ADDb.

Settlement Option for Maturity

1. What is Settlement Option?

The Settlement Option (also called Surrender Value Option or Maturity Payout Option) is a facility where, instead of receiving the maturity proceeds in a single lump sum, the policyholder chooses to receive it in installments over a period of time.

  • Options: 5, 10, or 15 years.
  • Frequency: Monthly, Quarterly, Half-yearly, or Yearly.
  • Benefit: Interest is paid on the remaining amount held by LIC, making the total payout higher than the lump sum.

2. Why is it Needed?

  • Avoid Misuse: Prevents the risk of spending the entire lump sum immediately on impulsive purchases.
  • Regular Income Stream: Acts as a pension for retired people or those with fixed income needs.
  • Financial Discipline: Spreads the payout over time while keeping the principal safe with the insurer.

3. How it Works – Mathematical Example

Assumptions:
Maturity Value: ₹10,00,000
Settlement Period: 5 Years
Interest Rate: 6% p.a. (Illustrative)
Mode: Annual Installment
Step 1: Calculate Installment
Formula: Installment = [Maturity × (1 + rate × time)] / time

= [10,00,000 × (1 + 0.06 × 5)] / 5
= [10,00,000 × 1.30] / 5
= 13,00,000 / 5
= ₹2,60,000 per year

Step 2: Total Payout Comparison
Total Received via Settlement: ₹13,00,000
Original Lump Sum: ₹10,00,000
Gain: ₹3,00,000 extra via interest.

4. Comparison: Lump Sum vs Settlement

Option Payout Structure Pros Cons
Lump Sum ₹10,00,000 (Immediate) Full access immediately; freedom to invest elsewhere. Risk of spending it all; no interest earned from LIC.
Settlement ₹2.6L/year for 5 years (Total ₹13L) Regular income; reduced misuse risk; guaranteed interest. Less liquidity; cannot withdraw full amount in emergency.

5. Is it Better than Lump Sum?

✅ Better If: You need regular income (post-retirement), want financial discipline, or fear wasting the money.

❌ Not Better If: You need large immediate funds (e.g., buying a house, business capital) or can invest better than LIC's rate yourself.

Summary: Settlement Option provides an income stream, prevents misuse, and adds interest to your payout. It is ideal for retirees but limits immediate liquidity.

Choice of Term / PPT / SA

To maximize Savings on Premium and ensure High Maturity Returns, we must analyze the interaction between Payment Mode, Premium Paying Term (PPT), Policy Term (PT), and Sum Assured (SA).

1. Theoretical Analysis: The 4 Variables

A. Payment Mode

Single Premium: You pay once. Full money is invested immediately. No administrative cost for collection.
Result: Highest Maturity Returns.

Yearly (Yly): Lowest premium loading among regular modes.
Result: Better returns than Monthly/Quarterly.

Monthly (Mly) / Quarterly (Qly): Higher frequency = Mode Loading charges apply. Slightly reduces effective maturity.

✅ Rule: Single > Annual > Half-Yearly > Quarterly > Monthly

B. Premium Paying Term (PPT)

Limited PPT (e.g., 5-10 yrs): Higher annual premium, but paid for fewer years. Total premium paid is usually lower than full term.
Result: Higher effective return.

Full PPT (Equal to Term): Lower annual premium, but paid for many years.
Result: Slightly lower effective return.

✅ Rule: Low/Limited PPT > Full PPT for returns.

C. Policy Term (PT)

High PT (e.g., 20+ yrs): Longer time for bonus to accumulate and compound.
Result: Higher Maturity Value.

Low PT (e.g., 10 yrs): Quick maturity, but less time for growth.

✅ Rule: High PT > Low PT.

D. Sum Assured (SA)

Higher SA leads to higher absolute maturity. Choose the highest SA that is affordable to maximize wealth creation.

2. Mathematical Examples (Proof of Concept)

Assumptions: SA ₹10 Lakhs (or ₹15L), Bonus 6% Simple Interest (Illustrative).

Scenario 1: Full Term (Standard)
SA: ₹10L | PT: 15 Yrs | PPT: 15 Yrs | Mode: Annual
Annual Premium: ₹50,000
Total Paid: ₹50,000 × 15 = ₹7,50,000
Bonus: ₹9,00,000
Maturity: ₹19,00,000
Gain: ₹11.5 Lakhs
IRR: ~7.2%
Scenario 2: Limited PPT (Better)
SA: ₹10L | PT: 15 Yrs | PPT: 10 Yrs | Mode: Annual
Annual Premium: ₹65,000
Total Paid: ₹65,000 × 10 = ₹6,50,000
Bonus: ₹9,00,000 (Same as Scen 1)
Maturity: ₹19,00,000
Gain: ₹12.5 Lakhs
IRR: ~9.1% ✅ (Better Return, Less Payment)
Scenario 3: The Jackpot Strategy (High PT + Limited PPT)
SA: ₹15L | PT: 20 Yrs | PPT: 10 Yrs | Mode: Annual
Annual Premium: ₹97,500
Total Paid: ₹97,500 × 10 = ₹9,75,000
Bonus: ₹18,00,000 (Accrued over 20 years)
Maturity: 15L + 18L = ₹33,00,000
Gain: ₹23.25 Lakhs
IRR: ~11.4% 🏆 (Best Choice)
Scenario 4: The Monthly Trap
Same as Scenario 2, but Monthly Mode.
Due to mode loading, you pay extra annually (~₹51,600 total).
Total Paid increases → IRR drops to ~8.9%.

3. Practical Recommendation

Factor Recommended Choice Why?
Payment Mode Single > Annual Avoids mode loading; money grows longer.
PPT Limited (Low) Pay for fewer years; lower total cost; higher yield.
Policy Term High (Long) Maximize bonus accumulation/compounding.
Sum Assured High (Affordable) Maximizes the final absolute payout.
Winning Formula: Limited PPT + High Policy Term + High Sum Assured + Annual Mode = Maximum Savings & Highest Maturity.

Customer Scenarios: Finding Your Perfect Fit

Different customers have different financial capabilities and goals. Below is a detailed breakdown of how to choose Policy Term (PT), Premium Paying Term (PPT), Sum Assured (SA), and Payment Mode for 6 specific types of customers.

1. The "Budget Conscious" (Affordability First)

Goal: Affordable annual cash outflow, moderate coverage, disciplined savings.

  • Strategy: Medium Term (10-15 yrs) + Full PPT + Low SA + Annual/Half-Yearly Mode.
  • Reason: Spreads premiums to reduce annual burden; avoids monthly loading.
Example:
SA: ₹5L | Term: 12 Yrs | PPT: 12 Yrs | Premium: ₹25,000/yr
Total Paid: ₹3,00,000
Maturity: ₹8,60,000
Verdict: Affordable, disciplined, safe.

2. The "Wealthy Investor" (Max Returns)

Goal: Maximize maturity; cash outflow is not an issue.

  • Strategy: High Term (20 yrs) + Limited PPT (10 yrs) + High SA + Annual/Single Mode.
  • Reason: Pay for fewer years to reduce cost; let money grow longer for max bonus.
Example:
SA: ₹15L | Term: 20 Yrs | PPT: 10 Yrs | Premium: ₹1.2L/yr
Total Paid: ₹12,00,000
Maturity: ₹33,00,000
Verdict: High Premium → Massive Maturity (~11-12% IRR).

3. The "Quick Return" Seeker

Goal: Get money back in the shortest possible time.

  • Strategy: Low Term (10 yrs) + Low PPT (10 yrs) + Medium SA.
  • Reason: Minimize waiting period.
Example:
SA: ₹10L | Term: 10 Yrs | PPT: 10 Yrs | Premium: ₹80,000/yr
Total Paid: ₹8,00,000
Maturity: ₹16,00,000
Verdict: Fast returns, though bonus accumulation is lower.

4. The "Long-Term Believer"

Goal: Maximize wealth over a long horizon using compounding.

  • Strategy: High Term (20 yrs) + Limited PPT (10 yrs) + Medium SA.
  • Reason: Pay for a few years, then sit back and watch the bonus compound.
Example:
SA: ₹10L | Term: 20 Yrs | PPT: 10 Yrs | Premium: ₹65,000/yr
Total Paid: ₹6,50,000
Maturity: ₹22,00,000
Verdict: Best "Value for Money" strategy.

5. The "Middle Path" (Balanced)

Goal: Balance between affordability and moderate maturity.

  • Strategy: Medium Term (15 yrs) + Full PPT + Medium SA.
  • Reason: Comfortable premium, decent accumulation.
Example:
SA: ₹10L | Term: 15 Yrs | PPT: 15 Yrs | Premium: ₹50,000/yr
Total Paid: ₹7,50,000
Maturity: ₹19,00,000
Verdict: Disciplined savings, moderate growth.

6. The "Grand Maturity" Seeker

Goal: Highest possible maturity number.

  • Strategy: High Term (20 yrs) + Limited PPT + High SA + Single/Annual Mode.
  • Reason: Aggressive accumulation strategy.
Example:
SA: ₹15L | Term: 20 Yrs | PPT: 10 Yrs | Premium: ₹1.2L/yr
Total Paid: ₹12,00,000
Maturity: ₹33,00,000
Verdict: Grand payout achieved via high SA and time.
