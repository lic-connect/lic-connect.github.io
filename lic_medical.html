Important Definitions & Rules

Important Definitions & Rules

1. Standard Age Proof (SAP)

These are the "Gold Standard" documents. They allow for higher Maximum Sum Assured limits.

  • School/College Leaving Certificate (SSC/HSC)
  • Municipal Birth Certificate
  • Passport
  • PAN Card
  • Service Register (Govt/PSU/Defence)
  • Baptism Certificate

2. NSAP-I (Non-Standard I)

Accepted widely but restricts the Max Policy Term (usually to 25 years). Used when exact DOB is not on a "Standard" doc.

  • Driving License (Standard Issue)
  • ESI Card
  • Service Record (Private Limited Companies)
  • Marriage Certificate (if age is mentioned)

3. NSAP-II (Non-Standard II)

Secondary identification documents. Often attracts lower Sum Assured limits.

  • Aadhar Card (If full DOB is NOT verified or original not seen)
  • Voter ID Card (EPIC)
  • Ration Card (with name and age)

4. NSAP-III (Non-Standard III)

The weakest form of age proof. Allowed only for very small Sum Assured.

  • Self-Declaration (Standard Format)
  • Village Panchayat Certificate
  • Horoscope (sometimes treated as NSAP-III depending on plan)

5. Age Calculation Explained (NBD vs LBD)

The LIC Rule: Look at the gap between the Last Birthday and the Date of Proposal. If the gap is less than 6 months, stick to the lower age. If it is 6 months or more, jump to the next age.

Specific Example:
📅 Date of Birth: 15th Jan 1991
📝 Date of Proposal: 26th Jan 2026
1. LBD (Last Birthday): 35 Years
Why? The birthday that just passed was 15th Jan 2026.
(2026 - 1991 = 35). This is his completed age.
2. Next Birthday: 36 Years
Why? The upcoming birthday is 15th Jan 2027. He will turn 36 then.
3. NBD (Nearer Birthday): 35 Years
Why? Count the days from the Last Birthday (15th Jan) to Proposal Date (26th Jan).
Gap = 11 Days.
Since 11 days is less than 6 months, he is "Nearer" to 35 than he is to 36.
Final Age for Insurance = 35

6. SUC (Sum Under Consideration)

This is the figure used to determine Medical Tests. It is NOT just the current policy amount.

Formula:

(Rated SA of Current Proposal)
+
(Total SA of all policies taken in last 2 Full Years)

II. Part A: The New Business Medical Journey

The medical journey for a new proposal is initiated the moment the system identifies that a proposer falls outside the "Non-Medical" grid based on Age, SUC (Sum Under Consideration), or adverse health disclosures in the proposal form. This process is governed by strict IRDAI and LIC internal guidelines to ensure the life is insurable at the time of entry.

Step-by-Step Breakdown:
  • Proposal Initiation: The journey begins with the completion of the Proposal Form (Form 300 or 340). Here, the life assured must practice Uberrima Fides (Utmost Good Faith), disclosing every known health condition, smoking habits, and family longevity.
  • Requirement Generation: If the SUC exceeds the threshold for the age, the LIC system automatically generates a "Medical Requirement." This usually consists of a Medical Examiner’s Report (MER) and sometimes Special Reports like a Lipid Profile, HbA1c, or Chest X-Ray.
  • The Medical Examination: The proposer must visit a Panel Doctor. LIC maintains a panel of authorized MBBS/MD doctors. A medical report from a non-panel doctor is strictly invalid. The doctor’s primary role is identity verification—checking the photo ID against the person being examined to prevent "proxy" medicals.
  • Underwriting Review: Once submitted, an Underwriter (at the Branch, Division, or Central level) reviews the MER. If the MER shows borderline BP or minor abnormalities, the Underwriter may call for an FMR (Full Medical Report) or further specialist opinions.

III. Part B: The Revival Medical Journey

Revival is the process of putting a lapsed policy back into force. Unlike a new proposal, the contract already exists, but the risk has paused. The focus of a medical journey in revival is not "Initial Insurability" but "Continued Insurability."

The Logic of Revival Medicals:
  • Decision Matrix: If a policy has been lapsed for more than 6 months, LIC typically asks for a DGH (Declaration of Good Health). However, if the lapse period is long (2+ years) or the policyholder has reached a higher age bracket, a full MER is triggered.
  • Is the Life still Insurable? The Underwriter looks at the medical reports to see if any new habits (smoking/alcohol) or diseases (Diabetes/Hypertension) have developed during the lapse period.
  • Section 45 Clause: Note that for revivals, the "Indisputability Clause" does not usually restart from scratch, though fraudulent non-disclosure in the revival medical can still lead to claim repudiation.

IV. Critical Roles & Responsibilities

1. Role of the LIC Agent (The Facilitator)

The agent is the bridge but must never interfere with the medical process. They are responsible for coordinating the appointment and explaining fasting requirements (usually 10-12 hours for blood tests). **Strict Prohibition:** An agent is prohibited by LIC rules from being present inside the doctor's chamber during the physical examination. They must not fill the MER or suggest answers to the doctor.

2. Role of the LIC Panel Doctor

The doctor acts as the "Eyes and Ears" of the Underwriter. They must record clinical findings objectively—BMI, Pulse rate, Heart sounds, and Abdominal tenderness. They are required to verify the proposer’s signature on the MER to ensure document integrity.

3. Role of the Diagnostic Lab

For special tests (TMT, Echo, Blood work), LIC-approved labs must be used. These labs directly upload reports to the LIC portal or provide them in a sealed envelope to maintain a "Chain of Custody," preventing the tampering of results by the proposer or agent.

VI. DGH: The Declaration of Good Health

The DGH is a unique document. It is NOT a medical report by a doctor; it is a self-certification by the policyholder. By signing a DGH, the proposer legally states: "Since my last statement, I have not suffered from any illness, have not been hospitalized, and have not consulted any doctor." If a DGH is signed while the proposer is aware of a hidden illness, it is considered a material misstatement and can lead to the death claim being rejected later.

VII. The Golden Underwriting Rule

"The medical journey is designed to protect the Life Fund." LIC does not conduct medicals to find reasons to reject a policy, but to find the correct Price of Risk. If a medical report shows a health issue, the journey ends with a "rating"—an extra premium that allows the person to still have life insurance while covering the increased risk for the corporation.

Whether it is a new proposal or a revival case, the journey ensures that the person being insured is the same person who signed the form, and their health matches the premium being paid.
