Important Definitions & Rules

Important Definitions & Rules

1. Standard Age Proof (SAP)

These are the "Gold Standard" documents. They allow for higher Maximum Sum Assured limits.

  • School/College Leaving Certificate (SSC/HSC)
  • Municipal Birth Certificate
  • Passport
  • PAN Card
  • Service Register (Govt/PSU/Defence)
  • Baptism Certificate

2. NSAP-I (Non-Standard I)

Accepted widely but restricts the Max Policy Term (usually to 25 years). Used when exact DOB is not on a "Standard" doc.

  • Driving License (Standard Issue)
  • ESI Card
  • Service Record (Private Limited Companies)
  • Marriage Certificate (if age is mentioned)

3. NSAP-II (Non-Standard II)

Secondary identification documents. Often attracts lower Sum Assured limits.

  • Aadhar Card (If full DOB is NOT verified or original not seen)
  • Voter ID Card (EPIC)
  • Ration Card (with name and age)

4. NSAP-III (Non-Standard III)

The weakest form of age proof. Allowed only for very small Sum Assured.

  • Self-Declaration (Standard Format)
  • Village Panchayat Certificate
  • Horoscope (sometimes treated as NSAP-III depending on plan)

5. Age Calculation Explained (NBD vs LBD)

The LIC Rule: Look at the gap between the Last Birthday and the Date of Proposal. If the gap is less than 6 months, stick to the lower age. If it is 6 months or more, jump to the next age.

Specific Example:
📅 Date of Birth: 15th Jan 1991
📝 Date of Proposal: 26th Jan 2026
1. LBD (Last Birthday): 35 Years
Why? The birthday that just passed was 15th Jan 2026.
(2026 - 1991 = 35). This is his completed age.
2. Next Birthday: 36 Years
Why? The upcoming birthday is 15th Jan 2027. He will turn 36 then.
3. NBD (Nearer Birthday): 35 Years
Why? Count the days from the Last Birthday (15th Jan) to Proposal Date (26th Jan).
Gap = 11 Days.
Since 11 days is less than 6 months, he is "Nearer" to 35 than he is to 36.
Final Age for Insurance = 35

6. SUC (Sum Under Consideration)

This is the figure used to determine Medical Tests. It is NOT just the current policy amount.

Formula:

(Rated SA of Current Proposal)
+
(Total SA of all policies taken in last 2 Full Years)

Note: Exclude Term Rider SA unless specified.

II. Part A: The Modern LIC Medical Toolbox

In the digital age, LIC has evolved its medical underwriting from simple physical checkups to a multi-tiered diagnostic ecosystem. This system is designed to balance proposer convenience with risk accuracy. Below are the core components used today:

1. Tele-MER (Tele-Medical Examination Report)
  • What it is: A structured medical interview conducted over a recorded phone call by an LIC-authorized doctor.
  • When used: Primarily for "Low Risk" proposals—younger age groups and moderate Sum Under Consideration (SUC).
  • Why: It eliminates the need for physical travel while ensuring a medical professional verifies the health declarations.
  • Cost: Borne entirely by LIC.
2. Video-MER (Video-Medical Examination Report)
  • What it is: A live video call between the doctor and the proposer using a secure platform.
  • When used: "Medium Risk" cases, high-value policies where the proposer is in a remote location, or for NRI (Non-Resident Indian) cases.
  • Why: It allows the doctor to verify the proposer's identity visually (preventing dummy medicals) and observe physical gait, skin condition, and breathlessness.
  • Cost: Borne entirely by LIC.
3. Third-Party MSPs (Medical Service Providers)
  • What it is: Corporate diagnostic chains (like Apollo, Healthians, or SRL) empanelled by LIC.
  • When used: For high-value policies requiring multiple diagnostic tests (Blood, ECG, X-Ray).
  • Why: To ensure "Chain of Custody." MSPs upload results directly to LIC's portal, preventing agents or proposers from tampering with the reports.
  • Cost: LIC pays for New Business; Proposer pays for Revivals.
4. LIC Panel Doctor (Traditional)
  • What it is: Individual MBBS/MD doctors authorized to conduct physical exams at their private clinics.
  • When used: Where MSP coverage is unavailable or for specific physical examinations like MER/FMR.
  • Cost: LIC pays for New Business; Proposer pays for Revivals.

III. Part B: Technical Terminologies & Triggers

Underwriters use specific codes and report types to assess the "Price of Risk." Understanding these is vital for any insurance professional or policyholder.

Term Full Form & Description Key Observations
MER Medical Examiner's Report: Basic physical check (Height, Weight, BP, Pulse, Heart sounds). Triggers for most standard medical cases.
FMR Full Medical Report: A deeper systemic review including Central Nervous System (CNS) and Abdominal palpation. Triggers for very high SUC or when MER shows abnormalities.
DGH Declaration of Good Health: A self-signed form by the policyholder stating no health changes. Used for Revivals within specific time frames. Not a doctor's report.
SBT-13 Serum Biochemistry Test (13 Parameters): Comprehensive blood profile (Liver, Kidney, Glucose). Standard for high SA cases to check internal organ health.
CTMT Computerized Treadmill Test: Heart stress test while walking/running on a treadmill. Triggers for older ages or high SUC to detect silent heart issues.
HbA1c Glycated Hemoglobin: Measures average blood sugar over the last 3 months. Mandatory if the proposer is diabetic or has borderline fasting sugar.

IV. Part C: The New Business Medical Journey

The journey of a new proposal is governed by the principle of Uberrima Fides (Utmost Good Faith). Here is the granular step-by-step process:

  1. Financial & Age Scrubbing: Once the proposal form (Form 300) is submitted, the LIC system calculates the SUC. If the SUC exceeds the Non-Medical limit for that age/profession, "Medical Requirements" are auto-generated.
  2. Appointment Coordination: If assigned to an MSP, the proposer receives an SMS link to choose a slot. If assigned to a Panel Doctor, the agent coordinates the visit.
    • Role of Agent: To guide the proposer on "Pre-Examination Readiness" (e.g., 12-hour fasting for SBT-13).
  3. Identity Verification: The Doctor/Technician MUST verify the original Aadhar/Passport. Note: This is where most fraud is caught.
  4. The Examination: The doctor conducts the MER/FMR. Strict Rule: No third party (Agent/Family) should be in the room to ensure privacy and honest answers.
  5. Direct Submission: In the modern system, the doctor/lab uploads the digital report. The proposer no longer carries a physical envelope to the LIC branch (to prevent tampering).
  6. The Decision:
    • Standard: Accepted at normal rates.
    • Sub-Standard: Accepted with "Loading" (Extra premium due to health risks like BMI or BP).
    • Postponed: E.g., if the proposer just had surgery, LIC may wait 6 months.
    • Declined: If the risk is too high (e.g., critical heart blockage).

Cost Ownership: In New Business, LIC views medicals as its own "cost of doing business." LIC bears the expense of all primary tests it demands. However, if a proposer wants a re-test because they claim the first was wrong, the proposer must pay for the second test.

V. Part D: The Revival Medical Journey

Revival is the "re-birth" of a lapsed policy. The medical requirements here are stricter because the "Continuity of Risk" has been broken.

1. The "DGH" Window (0-6 Months Lapse)

Usually, a simple Form 680 (DGH) is enough. The proposer signs a legal declaration. If they hide a hospitalization that occurred during the lapse, the claim will be rejected later under Section 45 of the Insurance Act.

2. The Medical Window (6+ Months or High Age)

If the policy has been lapsed for a long time, the system ignores Non-Medical privileges. Even a small SA may require a full MER. This is to ensure the person hasn't developed a chronic illness while the policy was "dead."

3. Professional Ethics in Revival

Agents often try to push for "Special Revivals" to avoid medicals. However, the system-driven logic now makes this difficult. Any "Adverse Feature" noted in the DGH (e.g., "I had a minor chest pain") will immediately convert the case from DGH to a full Medical requirement.

Cost Ownership: Unlike New Business, the Proposer bears the full cost of medicals for Revival. LIC's logic is that the policyholder failed to pay premiums on time, so the corporation should not be penalized for the cost of re-assessing the risk.

VI. The Final Word: Ethics & Protection

The role of the LIC Medical Examiner (Panel Doctor or MSP) is to act as a "Gatekeeper." When an agent and doctor collude to sign a "Ghost Medical" (without seeing the patient), they aren't just breaking rules—they are invalidating the policyholder's protection.

Three Golden Rules for a Safe Policy:
  1. Never Bypass Medicals: If the system asks for a test, take it. A policy issued after a full medical is nearly "bulletproof" against claim rejection after 3 years.
  2. Demand Digital Upload: Ensure your reports are uploaded directly to the LIC system. Avoid physical handling of reports.
  3. Disclosure is Cheaper than Rejection: Paying a 10% "Extra Premium" for High BP is better than having your family's 100% claim rejected for non-disclosure.

The medical underwriting process is the foundation of the Life Fund. It ensures that honest policyholders do not pay for the risks of those who hide terminal illnesses.

VII. The Golden Underwriting Rule

"The medical journey is designed to protect the Life Fund." LIC does not conduct medicals to find reasons to reject a policy, but to find the correct Price of Risk. If a medical report shows a health issue, the journey ends with a "rating"—an extra premium that allows the person to still have life insurance while covering the increased risk for the corporation.

Whether it is a new proposal or a revival case, the journey ensures that the person being insured is the same person who signed the form, and their health matches the premium being paid.
