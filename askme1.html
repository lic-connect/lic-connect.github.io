1. What should I do if my original LIC policy bond is lost?

The Legal Procedure

Losing an LIC bond is treated as a serious legal matter because the bond is a "Document of Title." It establishes your ownership and is required for all financial transactions like loans or claims.

Offline Process (Mandatory): You must immediately notify your servicing branch in writing to freeze any unauthorized transactions. Following this, you must lodge a Police FIR containing the policy number and plan details. Next, LIC requires a Newspaper Advertisement in a local daily (English or Regional) declaring the loss. Finally, you must execute an Indemnity Bond on non-judicial stamp paper to protect LIC against future disputes if the original bond is misused.

Online Status: Currently, this process is not available online. While you can register a query on the portal, the submission of the FIR, the original newspaper cutting, and the stamped Indemnity Bond must be done physically at the branch for legal verification in the ANANDA system.

2. How can I obtain a duplicate LIC policy bond?

Formal Replacement Process

Obtaining a duplicate bond is a formal replacement process, not a simple reprint. LIC must satisfy itself that the original cannot be used for fraudulent claims or double-financing.

Offline Procedure: You must submit a formal application at your servicing branch. LIC officials will then perform a Scrutiny through the ANANDA system to check for existing assignments, pending loans, or maturity statuses. Once cleared, you must pay the Duplicate Bond Fee, which is calculated based on the Sum Assured (SA) of your policy. The new bond is then printed with a clear stamp: “Duplicate Policy Bond issued in lieu of original.”

Online Status: Duplicate bond issuance is strictly offline. Because it involves paying a variable fee and verifying legal instruments like an Indemnity Bond, the portal cannot yet automate the physical marking and issuance of a title document. Agents can guide you through the paperwork, but they cannot sign these legal documents on your behalf.

3. Documents required for Duplicate Bond?

Document Checklist

LIC insists on original legal instruments to prevent impersonation or "Anti-Selection" (where a policyholder tries to get a duplicate despite having assigned the original to a third party).

Offline Requirements: You must provide a Police FIR copy clearly stating the policy number. You also need the Original Newspaper Cutting from a widely circulated daily. The most critical document is the Indemnity Bond, which must be executed on non-judicial stamp paper (the value varies by state). Additionally, standard KYC (Aadhaar/PAN) must match the policy records perfectly. In some high Sum Assured cases, LIC may also require a Surety—a person of financial standing who signs the indemnity along with you.

Online Status: Scanned uploads are not sufficient for this transaction. Since the Indemnity Bond is a physical legal instrument that LIC must hold in its archives to defend against future litigation, you must submit the physical "Duplicate Kit" to the branch manager.

4. Legal value of a duplicate policy bond?

Legal Standing

Once issued, a duplicate bond has exactly the same legal validity as the original. It becomes the "Sole Document of Title" for all future contractual interactions between you and the corporation.

Offline/System Impact: The moment a duplicate is issued, the ANANDA Master Record is updated. The original bond number is marked as "Cancelled" or "Replaced." This means if someone tries to take a loan using your original lost bond, the system will flag it as invalid. It allows you to perform all actions such as Absolute Assignment, Policy Loans, or Surrender.

Legal Liability: It is important to note that a duplicate bond is issued based on your Personal Indemnity. If the original bond resurfaces and a third party proves they had a legal right to it (like a previous assignment), the financial liability falls on you, not LIC. Online, the portal will only recognize the duplicate's details for future servicing requests.

5. What if original bond is found later?

Finding the Original

If you discover the original bond after the duplicate has been issued, the original bond loses all legal status immediately. It is effectively a worthless piece of paper in the eyes of the law.

Offline Protocol: You are contractually obligated to inform the servicing branch and Surrender the original bond for destruction or cancellation. Attempting to use the original bond for a loan or claim while a duplicate exists is treated as misrepresentation or fraud under the Insurance Act. LIC will check the "Duplicate Flag" in the ANANDA system before any payout.

Online Reality: The Customer Portal will only display the "Duplicate Issued" status. Even if you have the original in hand, you cannot use its details to link the policy or perform online actions if those details conflict with the duplicate record. Your personal legal liability remains active until the original is physically surrendered to the corporation.

6. How can I download a soft copy?

Digital Access (E-Policy)

LIC provides access to a digitally generated version of your policy details for convenience, but this is not a legal replacement for the physical bond in "title-based" transactions.

Online Process: You must register on the LIC Customer Portal. After logging in, navigate to the "Policy Details" or "E-Policy" section. From here, you can download a PDF version of your policy. This is excellent for personal record-keeping, verifying your bonus accrual, or discussing your portfolio with an agent.

Offline/Legal Limitation: You cannot use this soft copy to apply for a loan at a branch or to assign the policy to a bank. Branches do not generally email these copies; they must be accessed through the secure portal to ensure data privacy. For any legal action (Surrender/Claim), the physical bond (Original or Duplicate) remains mandatory. The soft copy is essentially a digital reflection of the ANANDA database for your information only.

7. How to register on Customer Portal?

Online Registration

The LIC Customer Portal is the primary gateway for self-servicing. Registering here empowers you to manage your policies without visiting a branch.

Online Process: Visit the official LIC website and click on "New User." You will need to provide your Policy Number, Date of Birth (matching the bond), Mobile Number, and Email. Verification is dual-layered: an OTP is sent to both your phone and email. Once verified, you create a password and gain access to premium receipts, loan status, and nomination details.

Offline Prerequisite: Registration will fail if your mobile number or DOB does not match the ANANDA records. In such cases, you must first visit the branch to submit a "Contact Update Form." Once the branch updates the master record, you can complete the online registration. This ensures that only the actual policyholder can access sensitive financial data.

8. If I forget my policy number?

Recovery Mechanisms

Losing your policy number is a common hurdle, but LIC provides multiple ways to retrieve it using your demographic data.

Online Method: On the login page, use the "Forgot Policy Number" link. You will be asked for your registered mobile number, Date of Birth, and PAN. After a successful OTP verification, the system will display the policy numbers linked to your profile. This is the fastest way to recover data.

Offline Method: If your mobile number is also not registered, you must visit any LIC branch. Provide your Aadhaar or PAN card. The staff can perform a "Bio-data Search" in ANANDA using your Name and DOB combination. To prevent identity theft, the branch will not provide the number over the phone. Practical Tip: Always keep a digital photo of your bond in your email or cloud storage for such emergencies.

9. Multiple policies in one login?

Consolidated View

LIC allows you to manage all your policies—whether Endowment, Money Back, or Term—under a Single Unified Dashboard.

Online Sync: Most policies are linked automatically via your Unique Customer ID in the ANANDA system, provided your Date of Birth and PAN are identical across all proposals. If a policy is missing, you can use the "Add Policy" feature by entering the policy number. It will be added after a quick validation of your DOB.

Offline Consistency: If you cannot add a policy online, it usually means there is a Data Mismatch in the branch records (e.g., your name is "Amit Kumar" in one and "A. Kumar" in another). You must visit the branch to "merge" these records into a single Customer ID. Note that you can only link policies where you are the Proposer; policies of family members usually require separate logins for security.

10. Details required to link policies?

Data Consistency

Linking a policy to your digital account is a security-sensitive process. It relies on a perfect 1:1 match between your input and the ANANDA master record.

Required Details: You must provide the Policy Number, Exact Date of Birth (as printed on the bond), and the Registered Mobile Number. Even a minor discrepancy in the DOB (day/month swap) will result in a linking failure.

Offline Correction: If linking fails repeatedly, it is a signal of a backend data error. You must visit your servicing branch with an ID proof (Aadhaar/PAN). The staff will check the Bio-data segment in ANANDA. If your name or DOB was entered incorrectly during the manual data-entry phase years ago, they will correct it. Once the master record is "Cleaned," the online linking will work instantly. This process ensures that your financial dashboard cannot be hijacked by someone with similar personal details.

11. How can I add or change a nominee in my LIC policy?

The Nomination Process

Nomination is a critical process governed by Section 39 of the Insurance Act, 1938. It ensures that LIC knows exactly who to pay the claim proceeds to in the event of your death. It is important to note that a nominee is a "receiver" and not necessarily the "owner" of the money, acting as a trustee for legal heirs.

Offline Process: You must fill out Form No. 3750. You need to provide the nominee's full name, relationship to you, age, and percentage share. If the nominee is a minor, you must appoint an "Appointee" (an adult who will receive the money on the minor's behalf). This form, along with the original policy bond, is submitted to the servicing branch for a physical endorsement.

Online Process: For registered users, this can be initiated via the LIC Customer Portal. Under "Profile Services," you can select "Nomination Change." The system uses Aadhaar-based OTP or registered mobile OTP to authenticate the request. Once updated, a digital confirmation is generated in the ANANDA system. You can change your nominee any number of times; the latest registration automatically cancels all previous ones.

12. Difference between Nomination and Assignment?

Ownership vs. Payout

Understanding the legal distinction between Nomination (Section 39) and Assignment (Section 38) is vital for estate planning. They represent two different levels of legal rights over the policy contract.

Nomination (Offline/Online): This is merely a direction to LIC regarding the payout recipient. The policyholder remains the sole owner of the policy. You can still take a loan, surrender the policy, or change the nominee without anyone's permission. The nominee’s right is an "expectancy" that only matures upon your death.

Assignment (Offline Only): This is a transfer of title. Once you assign a policy, the "Assignee" (the person or bank you gave it to) becomes the legal owner. You lose the right to take a loan or surrender the policy; only the Assignee can do that. Assignment can be "Absolute" (permanent) or "Conditional" (temporary, like a bank loan). Legally, Assignment is superior; if a policy is assigned, the previous nomination is automatically cancelled. This process requires a physical endorsement on the policy bond and cannot be completed purely online due to the transfer of property rights.

13. What happens to nomination after assignment?

The Overriding Rule

The Insurance Act provides clear guidance on how nomination behaves when a policy is used as collateral or transferred to another party. Generally, a transfer of ownership (Assignment) renders a previous nomination null and void.

Absolute Assignment: If you assign your policy to another person permanently (e.g., as a gift or sale), the nomination is cancelled immediately. LIC will only pay the Assignee or their heirs. To restore a nominee, the policy would have to be reassigned back to you first.

Conditional Assignment (Offline): This is common when you take a loan from a bank or LIC itself. If assigned to a bank, the nomination is usually cancelled. However, there is a Legal Exception: if the policy is assigned to LIC as security for a loan, the nomination is not cancelled but only "suspended." Once the loan is repaid and the policy is reassigned to you, the original nomination revives automatically in the ANANDA records. Because this involves legal title changes, all such updates require the physical submission of the policy bond to the branch for "Re-assignment" endorsement; it cannot be done via the Customer Portal.

14. Can I appoint more than one nominee?

Sharing the Payout

LIC provides the flexibility to appoint Multiple Nominees to ensure that your family’s financial needs are divided according to your specific wishes. This is particularly useful for providing separate shares to a spouse, children, or parents.

Offline Process: When filling out Form 3750, you must list all nominees and specify the Percentage Share for each. For example, you can assign 50% to your spouse and 25% each to two children. It is mandatory that the total sum of all shares equals exactly 100%. If any nominee is a minor, you must name an appointee for that specific minor.

Online Process: Through the Customer Portal, the "Nomination Change" section allows you to "Add Rows" for multiple nominees. The system will validate that the "Percentage Column" totals 100 before allowing the OTP submission. Legal Note: If death occurs, LIC will issue separate payments (NEFT) to each nominee based on their recorded ratio. If no share is mentioned in an old policy, LIC may treat the nominees as "Joint Holders" with equal shares, but current ANANDA rules mandate explicit percentages to avoid legal disputes.

15. Change nominee after marriage or divorce?

Legal Realities

It is a common misconception that major life events like marriage or divorce automatically update insurance records. Legally, LIC is only bound by the written nomination currently existing in its ANANDA database, regardless of your current marital status.

The Risk: If you divorce and do not change your nominee, your ex-spouse remains legally entitled to receive the claim proceeds upon your death. LIC will not intervene in family disputes; they will pay the person whose name is on the record. Similarly, after marriage, your parents might remain as nominees unless you manually add your spouse.

Process (Online/Offline): You should use Form 3750 (Offline) or the Customer Portal (Online) to update these details. While marriage/divorce certificates are generally not required for a simple nomination change, they are vital if you are also changing your own surname in the policy records. Updating the nomination is a Self-Service action that must be prioritized during life transitions to ensure your current family's financial security is protected from legal complications.

16. What if the nominee dies before me?

Pre-deceased Nominee

If the person you nominated dies during your lifetime, the nomination becomes Legally Ineffective. LIC does not automatically "move" the nomination to the nominee's heirs; the right simply expires.

Consequences of Inaction (Offline): If you do not register a new nominee and you also pass away, the policy is treated as a "No Nomination" case. This is a nightmare for your family. To receive the money, your heirs will have to approach a Civil Court to obtain a Succession Certificate or a Legal Heir Certificate. This involves court fees, legal delays of 6–12 months, and significant emotional stress.

Correct Action (Online/Offline): The moment a nominee passes away, you must submit Form 3750 at the branch or use the Customer Portal to appoint a new nominee. If you have multiple nominees and one dies, you should re-distribute that person's percentage share among the survivors or add a new person. Regular "Policy Audits" by the policyholder are necessary to ensure that the ANANDA records reflect living, reachable individuals to ensure a fast-track claim settlement.

17. How to assign a policy to another person?

Transfer of Title

Assignment is a formal legal transfer of the Policy Contract as a piece of property. It is governed by Section 38 and is far more powerful than a nomination. It is typically used for gifting a policy to a spouse/child or selling it.

Offline Process (Mandatory): Assignment cannot be done online because it requires a "Wet Signature" and physical evidence of transfer on the policy bond. You must use Form No. 3850. Both the "Assignor" (current owner) and the "Assignee" (new owner) must sign the document in the presence of a witness. You must then submit the Original Policy Bond to the LIC branch. LIC will scrutinize the records in ANANDA to ensure there are no existing liens or loans before endorsing the new owner's name on the back of the bond.

Effect: Once the transfer is complete, the Assignor loses all rights. The Assignee now has the power to take a loan, surrender the policy for cash, or change the payout directions. This is a permanent transfer unless a "Re-assignment" is executed later. It is a vital tool for high-value estate planning and business partnerships.

18. Assigning policy to a bank for a loan?

Collateral Security

Using an LIC policy as collateral for a bank loan (Home Loan or Personal Loan) is a smart financial move. This is known as a Conditional Assignment, where the ownership is transferred to the bank only for the duration of the debt.

Offline Process: You must request the bank's "Assignment Form" or use LIC's standard format. The bank's authorized signatory and you will sign the documents, and the Original Policy Bond is handed over to the bank. The bank then sends the bond to LIC for registration in the ANANDA system. LIC will mark the bank as the "Assignee."

What happens during a Claim? If you pass away while the loan is active, LIC will first pay the Outstanding Loan Amount to the bank. Any remaining balance is then paid to your nominee (if it was an assignment to LIC) or your legal heirs. Once the loan is fully repaid, the bank must execute a "Reassignment" endorsement. You must then submit this bond back to LIC to restore your ownership. Important: You cannot perform any online servicing like taking a policy loan while the bank holds the assignment.

19. Absolute vs Conditional Assignment?

Types of Ownership Transfer

The choice between Absolute and Conditional assignment depends on your intent: is it a gift or is it security for a debt? Both have distinct impacts on the ANANDA backend records.

Absolute Assignment: This is a total and permanent transfer. It is usually used when you want to transfer your wealth (e.g., assigning a policy to a child on their 18th birthday). The assignee becomes the owner for all time. All future bonuses and maturity proceeds belong solely to them. Offline: This requires clear wording in Form 3850 that the transfer is "Absolute."

Conditional Assignment: This is temporary. The ownership transfer is tied to a condition—usually the repayment of a loan or the assignee surviving the assignor. If the condition is met (loan repaid), the policy "reverts" back to the original owner. Online/Offline: While the assignment is active, you cannot perform online transactions. However, if the assignment was made to LIC for a policy loan, the Section 39 Nomination is not cancelled but merely suspended, making it easier to manage than a bank assignment. Both types require the physical bond for endorsement.

20. Can nomination be cancelled or revoked?

Right to Revoke

As the owner of the policy, you have the Absolute Legal Right to change your mind about who receives the money. A nominee has no "vested interest" in the policy while you are alive; they only have an "expectancy" of receiving money later.

Methods of Revocation: 1. Automatic Overriding: By simply registering a new nominee (Online via Portal or Offline via Form 3750), the previous nomination is automatically revoked. 2. Assignment: If you assign the policy to someone else, the old nomination is legally cancelled. 3. Explicit Revocation: You can submit a written request to the branch to cancel a nomination without appointing a new one (though this is not recommended as it creates a "No Nomination" case).

Online/Offline Validation: When you revoke or change a nomination, the ANANDA system timestamps the change. Only the latest recorded nomination is valid. If a policyholder dies, LIC will look at the date of the last successful update. If you revoke a nomination online, ensure you receive the "Success" confirmation and download the updated status for your records. This flexibility allows the policyholder to adapt their financial planning to changing life circumstances, such as family births or deaths.

21. How can I change my address in LIC records?

Updating Communication Records

Maintaining an accurate registered address is a fundamental requirement for policy servicing. LIC uses this address for all Statutory Communications, including premium notices, revival alerts, loan interest statements, and maturity intimation letters. If your address is outdated, you risk missing critical deadlines that could lead to policy lapse or financial loss.

Offline Process: You must visit your Servicing Branch (the branch where your policy records are maintained). You need to submit a signed request letter along with a self-attested photocopy of a valid address proof. The branch official will verify the original document and update the ANANDA database. This usually reflects in the system within 3 to 7 working days. For older "Legacy" policies, the branch visit is highly recommended to ensure the master data is correctly synced.

Online Process: Registered users on the LIC Customer Portal can update their address under "Profile Services." This is typically an Aadhaar-based process where the address is pulled directly from the UIDAI database via OTP authentication. If you are uploading a different proof (like a utility bill), the request will go through a central validation cell. Note that NRI / Foreign addresses cannot be updated online and require physical submission of documents at the branch to comply with international KYC and AML regulations.

22. What address proof documents are accepted by LIC?

Valid KYC Documents

LIC adheres to strict IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) guidelines. Any document used for an address change must be current, clearly legible, and the name on the proof must match the policyholder's name exactly as recorded in the ANANDA system.

Standard Documents (Offline/Online): The preferred documents are Aadhaar Cards and Passports because they serve as both identity and address proof. Other valid options include Voter ID cards and permanent Driving Licences. If you are using Utility Bills (Electricity, Water, or Piped Gas), ensure they are not older than 3 months from the date of submission. Bank Passbooks or statements are accepted only if they contain the current address and bear the Bank Official's Seal and Signature on the photograph page.

Special Logic: For online updates, LIC’s system performs an OCR (Optical Character Recognition) check. If the scan is blurry or the name has minor discrepancies (e.g., "S.K. Sharma" vs "Sunil Kumar Sharma"), the system will flag the request. In such cases, an offline visit is required where you may need to submit a Declaration of Identity to confirm that both names belong to the same individual. For rented premises, only a **Registered Rent Agreement** is accepted; a simple notarized affidavit is usually insufficient.

23. Can address be changed online in LIC policies?

Digital Limitations

While LIC has significantly expanded its digital footprint, the ability to change an address online is not universal for every policy. This is because the address is a key security field—changing it is often a precursor to fraudulent requests for duplicate bonds or policy loans by unauthorized persons.

Online Eligibility: You can successfully update your address via the Customer Portal if your policy is linked to your profile and you have a valid Aadhaar with a linked mobile number for OTP verification. This is limited to domestic Indian addresses. The ANANDA system will perform a real-time validation. Once successful, you will receive an automated confirmation email and SMS. This is the safest and fastest way to ensure your communication channel is open.

Offline Necessity: You cannot change your address online if: 1. You have a Foreign/NRI address; 2. Your policy is in a "Lapsed" state for a long duration; 3. The policy is a "Legacy" record (older manual entries); or 4. There is a pending legal dispute or assignment on the policy. In these scenarios, the portal will generate an error message or a "Visit Branch" prompt. This is a deliberate security feature to prevent identity theft. A physical visit allows the branch manager to perform a face-to-face verification before altering the master record.

24. How do I change my registered mobile number?

Critical Contact Link

In the age of digital banking and insurance, your Registered Mobile Number is the most important piece of data. It acts as the "Primary Key" for receiving 2FA (Two-Factor Authentication) OTPs, premium payment reminders, and tracking claim status. LIC’s ANANDA system uses this number to push real-time updates whenever a transaction occurs.

Online Process: If you still have access to your old mobile number, you can change it via the "Profile" section of the Customer Portal. The system will send an OTP to your current (old) number to verify that you are the one making the change. After this, an OTP is sent to the new number to complete the link. This "Double Handshake" ensures that no third party can hijack your digital account by simply changing the mobile number.

Offline Process: If you have lost your old phone or changed your number without updating the portal, the online method will fail because you cannot receive the initial OTP. In this case, a Physical Branch Visit is mandatory. You must submit a written request and a copy of your ID proof (Aadhaar/PAN). The branch staff will verify your signature against the original proposal form before updating the master record. This is a vital safeguard to prevent unauthorized persons from gaining control of your policy’s digital access.

25. Procedure to change Email ID in LIC?

Email & Digital Receipts

Registering your Email ID is the first step toward a paperless insurance experience. LIC uses your email to send E-Policy Bonds, digital premium receipts (valid for 80C tax deductions), and monthly newsletters. In some plans, LIC has stopped sending physical receipts altogether, making the email link essential for your financial records.

Online Process: This is the easiest update to perform. Log in to the LIC Customer Portal, navigate to "Profile Services," and select "Update Email." You simply enter the new email address and verify it via a Verification Link or OTP sent to that new email. Unlike mobile or address changes, LIC treats email updates as a "low-risk" transaction, meaning you generally don't need an OTP from your old email to verify the change.

Offline Process: If you are not a portal user, you can simply include your email ID in any written correspondence to the branch or fill out a simple "Contact Details Update Form." Once the email is updated in the ANANDA backend, all future premium receipts will be triggered automatically to your inbox. Practical Benefit: Having a registered email allows you to retrieve your policy number or reset your portal password much faster if you ever lose your physical documents.

26. How can I change bank account details?

Banking Integration

Since LIC has moved to a 100% Cheque-less environment for all payouts, having your correct bank details on file is the only way to receive Maturity, Survival Benefits, or Loan amounts. LIC uses the NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer) system to push money directly to your account, eliminating the risk of physical cheques being lost or stolen.

Offline Process: You must submit the NEFT Mandate Form (Form No. 3801). Along with this, you must attach a Cancelled Cheque that clearly shows your Name, Account Number, and IFSC Code printed on it. If your name is not printed on the cheque, you must provide a copy of your bank passbook with the bank's official seal. The branch will verify your signature against their records before updating the ANANDA payout gateway.

Online Process: Registered portal users can find the "Update Bank Details" option under the "Service Request" menu. You will need to upload a high-quality scan of your cancelled cheque. LIC often performs a "Penny Drop" verification, where they deposit Re. 1 into your account to confirm the name match before finalizing the update. This process takes about 7 to 10 working days. Crucial Tip: Never use a joint account unless you are the first holder, as it can lead to automated rejection during the NEFT process.

27. What is NEFT mandate and why is it compulsory?

Direct Benefit Transfer

The NEFT Mandate is a legal authorization provided by the policyholder to LIC, allowing the corporation to credit funds directly to a specific bank account. As per IRDAI regulations and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) norms, LIC has made NEFT registration Mandatory for every policy. The corporation no longer issues physical cheques for any benefit payment.

Why it is Compulsory: 1. Security: It prevents "Cheque Interception" where unauthorized persons could steal a cheque from the mail. 2. Transparency: Every payment has a UTR (Unique Transaction Reference) number, providing a permanent audit trail for both LIC and the customer. 3. Speed: There are no postal delays; the money reaches your account within 24 hours of the claim being approved in the ANANDA system.

Usage (Online/Offline): A single NEFT mandate can be applied to all policies held under the same Customer ID. Whether it is a Survival Benefit (Money Back), a Policy Loan, or the final Maturity Claim, the money will only be released if a verified NEFT record exists. If you haven't submitted your bank details, your maturity amount will be calculated but kept in a "Non-Interest Bearing" suspense account until the mandate is provided. Thus, submitting Form 3801 is the most important servicing task after buying a policy.

28. Can one bank account be used for multiple policies?

Account Consolidation

Yes, you can absolutely link one bank account to all LIC policies you own. In fact, LIC encourages this as it simplifies the management of your insurance portfolio. When you have multiple policies, having a single designated account ensures that you can easily track all survival benefits and maturity credits in one bank statement.

The Logic: The ANANDA system links policies based on your Customer ID. As long as the name on the bank account matches the name on the policy records exactly, the link is valid. You do not need to submit separate cancelled cheques for every policy. In the NEFT Mandate Form (3801), there is a section where you can list all your policy numbers to be linked to that specific account.

Online/Offline Linking: If you are using the Customer Portal, when you update your bank details for one policy, the system often asks if you would like to "Apply this to all policies." By selecting "Yes," the backend database is updated for your entire profile. Important Limitation: You cannot use your bank account for a policy where you are not the policyholder (e.g., your spouse's policy). LIC follows a strict "Self-to-Self" payment rule to prevent tax complications and unauthorized fund transfers.

29. If LIC payment fails due to wrong bank details?

Handling Payout Failures

A failed NEFT transaction is a common issue that occurs primarily due to Invalid IFSC codes (often after bank mergers), closed accounts, or a name mismatch where the bank's records do not align with LIC's records. When a payment fails, the money is not "lost"; it is automatically Reverted to LIC's Central Accounts.

The Process: Once the bank rejects the NEFT, the ANANDA system marks the policy status as "Payment Returned/Unpaid." LIC will then trigger an automated SMS and send a physical letter to your registered address requesting a Fresh NEFT Mandate. You cannot simply "retry" the same account; you must submit Form 3801 again with a new cancelled cheque to clear the error.

Financial Impact: It is important to note that LIC does not pay interest for the period of delay caused by incorrect bank details provided by the policyholder. The funds will sit in a "Suspense Account" until the new details are verified. Practical Tip: If your bank has recently merged (e.g., Syndicate Bank into Canara Bank), your old IFSC is now invalid. You must update your LIC records with the New IFSC immediately to avoid payout failures for upcoming survival benefits or maturity.

30. Can LIC pay maturity without bank details?

The Strict 'No Bank' Policy

Under current IRDAI and RBI Master Directions, life insurance companies are strictly prohibited from making payments in cash or via physical "Pay-to-order" cheques for any policy benefits. This is a non-negotiable part of the Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and "Know Your Customer" (KYC) framework designed to ensure all large financial transfers are traceable through the banking system.

The Result: If you reach your Maturity Date and have not submitted your bank details (NEFT mandate), LIC will Hold the Payment. The system will generate the maturity value and move it to a "Suspense Account." You will receive an intimation, but the funds will not be released. This holds true even if you visit the branch in person; the manager does not have the authority to bypass the NEFT requirement.

Offline/Online Requirement: To release the "Held" funds, you must submit Form 3801 at the branch or through the portal. Only after the bank details are successfully verified in ANANDA will the payment be triggered. Legal Exception: In very rare cases of a Court Order or an Absolute Legal Dispute where the claimant does not have a bank account, a physical cheque might be issued after high-level Zonal Office approval, but even then, it is usually made "Account Payee Only." For 99.9% of policyholders, No Bank Details = No Payout.

31. What is the grace period for LIC premium payment?

The Safety Buffer

The Grace Period is a vital contractual protection offered by LIC. It is an extension of time after the official Premium Due Date during which the policyholder can pay the premium without any late fee and, more importantly, without the policy losing its "In-force" status. This buffer is designed to handle temporary liquidity issues or postal delays.

Rules for Different Modes: 1. Monthly Mode: You get 15 Days of grace from the due date. 2. Quarterly, Half-Yearly, and Yearly Modes: You get 30 Days of grace. If the last day of grace is a public holiday, the next working day is considered the deadline. During this period, you can pay the premium Online or Offline without any interest penalty.

Risk Cover Continuity: This is the most critical legal feature. If the life assured passes away during the grace period, LIC treats the policy as Fully Active. The death claim is settled in full, though LIC will deduct the Unpaid Premium from the final settlement amount. Once the grace period expires, the policy is marked as "Lapsed" in the ANANDA system, and full risk cover is suspended. Therefore, the grace period is your "Last Safety Net" before the policy effectively dies.

32. What happens if I miss paying my LIC premium?

The Status Shift

Missing a premium beyond the grace period triggers a fundamental change in the legal contract between you and LIC. The ANANDA system automatically recalculates the policy status based on how many years of premiums you have already contributed. The consequences range from a total loss of benefits to a reduced continuation of the policy.

Scenario 1 (Lapse): If your policy is relatively new and you have Paid for less than 2 Full Years (for most modern plans), the policy Lapses Completely. The risk cover stops, no bonuses are added, and you cannot even surrender the policy for a refund. The money already paid is generally forfeited unless the policy is revived. This is the most dangerous state for a policyholder.

Scenario 2 (Paid-Up): If you have Paid for 2 or more Full Years, the policy becomes "Reduced Paid-Up." It doesn't die; instead, the Sum Assured is scaled down proportionally to the premiums paid. The risk cover continues at this lower amount until maturity, but you stop earning new bonuses. In both scenarios, the only way to restore the original "Full Risk" status is through the Revival Process, which involves paying the arrears with interest. Practical Tip: Always check your "FUP" (First Unpaid Premium) status on the portal to avoid falling into these categories.

33. When does an LIC policy lapse?

Defining 'Policy Death'

A policy Lapses officially the moment the grace period ends without a premium being recorded in the ANANDA database. This is a critical junction in the life of the insurance contract. For the corporation, a lapsed policy represents an "Expired Risk"—they are no longer receiving the price for the risk they are carrying.

The Consequences of Lapse: 1. Zero Risk Cover: If the policyholder dies even one day after the grace period, the death claim is usually rejected (unless it is a "Paid-up" case). 2. Loss of Bonus: LIC only shares its "Valuation Surplus" with In-force policies. A lapsed policy stops accumulating bonuses immediately. 3. Administrative Blocks: You cannot take a policy loan, change nomination online, or perform most servicing tasks on a lapsed policy. It is essentially a "frozen" account.

Checking Status: You can check if your policy has lapsed by looking at the Status field on the Customer Portal. If it says "Lapsed" instead of "In-force," you must act immediately. For policies issued after 2014, the "Paid-up" benefit only kicks in after 2 full years of payments. If you haven't reached that milestone, a lapse means you have no insurance protection at all. To "breathe life" back into the policy, you must initiate the Revival Procedure (see Q34).

34. How can I revive a lapsed LIC policy?

Restoring the Contract

Revival is the process of putting a lapsed policy back into "Full Force" (In-force) status. It is important to realize that revival is not a right, but a fresh invitation to contract. LIC has the right to reassess your health before accepting the arrears because the risk has already paused once.

Ordinary Revival (Offline): For policies lapsed for more than 6 months, you must pay all Unpaid Premiums plus Compounded Interest (currently around 9.5% p.a.). You must also submit a DGH (Declaration of Good Health). If the lapse is very long or the age is high, the ANANDA system will trigger a medical examination. You must visit the branch, get the "Revival Quote," and complete the medicals at a panel doctor.

Online Revival: For short-term lapses (usually less than 6 months), LIC often allows Online Revival through the portal. You can simply pay the arrears and late fee, and check a digital box for the DGH. This is the fastest way to restore cover. Once revived, all Past Bonuses that were missed are added back to the policy, and your full original Sum Assured is restored. This is always a better option than buying a new policy, as the premium for a new policy at an older age would be much higher.

35. What is the time limit available for revival?

The 5-Year Window

In a major pro-consumer move, the IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India) has standardized the revival period for traditional life insurance policies. For almost all LIC plans issued after 2014, the policyholder has a 5-Year Window to revive their lapsed policy.

Timeline Logic: This 5-year clock starts from the date of the FUP (First Unpaid Premium). For example, if your premium was due on 1st Jan 2022 and you didn't pay it, you have until 31st Dec 2026 to revive the policy. After this 5-year period, the policy enters "Permanent Discontinuance," and the contract is legally terminated. Your only remaining option would be to take the surrender value (if any).

The Cost of Waiting: While you have 5 years, the Late Fee (Interest) compounds every six months. Waiting until Year 4 or 5 to revive can make the interest amount almost as much as the premiums themselves. Additionally, as you age, the Medical Requirements become stricter. A policy that could have been revived with a simple DGH in Year 1 might require a Full Medical Report (FMR), ECG, and Blood Tests in Year 5. Therefore, early revival is both financially and medically beneficial.

36. What documents are required for LIC revival?

Revival Documentation

Because a revival is legally a "Re-underwriting" of the risk, LIC requires a specific set of documents to ensure that the risk hasn't changed significantly during the period the policy was lapsed. These documents are compiled into what agents call the "Revival Kit."

Standard Documents (Offline): 1. Form No. 680 or 700 (DGH): This is the health declaration form. 2. Original Policy Bond: Required for physical endorsement if the policy has been lapsed for a very long time. 3. KYC Update: If your address or mobile number has changed during the lapse period, fresh Aadhaar/PAN copies are mandatory. 4. Income Proof: For high Sum Assured revivals, LIC may ask for ITRs or salary slips to ensure you still have the "Insurable Interest" and the capacity to pay future premiums.

Digital Submission: For "Minor Revivals" (where the lapse is less than 6 months and the SA is moderate), the LIC Customer Portal allows you to complete the process without physical paperwork. You simply tick a digital health declaration and pay via UPI/Debit Card. However, for "Major Revivals" (long lapse or high age), the ANANDA system will generate a "Requirement Letter" which you must take to a branch or a panel doctor. Always keep a copy of your Revival Quote (provided by the branch) to ensure the total amount paid matches the interest calculation.

37. What is Declaration of Good Health (DGH)?

Self-Certified Medical Risk

The DGH (Declaration of Good Health) is a legal instrument where the policyholder self-certifies their current health status. Since LIC has not been "monitoring" your health during the lapse period, they rely on your "Utmost Good Faith" to disclose any new medical conditions or lifestyle changes (like smoking or alcohol consumption) that occurred while the policy was inactive.

What the Form Asks: The DGH (usually Form 680 or 700) asks specific, pointed questions: Have you been hospitalized? Have you undergone surgery? Do you suffer from Diabetes, Hypertension, or any heart condition? Have you consulted any doctor for more than 7 days? You must answer "Yes" or "No" truthfully. If you answer "Yes" to any question, the ANANDA system will automatically upgrade the revival to a Medical Revival, meaning you must undergo a physical checkup by a panel doctor.

The Legal Penalty for Lies: Signing a DGH with false information is considered Material Misrepresentation under the Insurance Act. If you hide a condition (like a heart surgery) during revival and die later, LIC will investigate your medical history. If they find you lied on the DGH, they have the legal right to Reject the Death Claim, even if the policy was "In-force" at the time of death. Therefore, truthfulness on the DGH is the only way to ensure the payout for your family.

38. What is Full Medical Report (FMR) in LIC?

Clinical Risk Assessment

An FMR (Full Medical Report) is a professional clinical evaluation conducted by an authorized LIC Panel Doctor. Unlike the DGH, which is your word, the FMR is the doctor's objective finding. It is triggered during revival or new business whenever the Sum Assured is high or the age of the proposer is above a certain threshold (usually 45 or 50).

The Examination Details: The panel doctor will record your Physical Stats (Height, Weight, BMI), Vital Signs (Blood Pressure, Pulse Rate), and perform a systemic examination of your heart, lungs, and abdomen. They will also look for visible signs of illness or surgical scars. The doctor must verify your identity using a photo ID to prevent "Proxy Medicals" (where a healthy person takes the test for a sick policyholder).

Underwriting Review: Once the doctor completes the FMR, the report is sent directly to the LIC Underwriting department. If the FMR shows a minor health issue (like borderline BP), LIC might still revive the policy but may charge an "Extra Premium" (Loading). If the health issue is severe, the revival may be "Postponed" or "Declined." You can find the list of authorized panel doctors at your branch or on the LIC portal. Remember, a report from your family doctor is Not Accepted for this purpose; it must be an LIC-empanelled professional.

39. Who bears the cost of medical tests during revival?

Cost Responsibility

One of the most common questions during a policy restoration is about the medical fees. There is a fundamental difference in how LIC handles medical costs for New Policies versus Revival Cases. This distinction is based on who "broke" the contract.

The Rule for Revival: In almost all revival cases, the Policyholder must bear the full cost of the medical examination (FMR) and any special tests (like ECG, Blood work, or X-rays). LIC’s logic is that the policy lapsed because the customer failed to pay the premium on time. Therefore, the corporation should not be financially penalized for the reassessment of a risk that was already active. You must pay the doctor or the diagnostic lab directly at the rates prescribed by LIC.

Contrast with New Business: When you buy a new policy, LIC usually pays the medical costs as part of its acquisition expense. However, even in new business, if the policy is not eventually completed, LIC might deduct the medical costs from the initial deposit. Special Campaigns: Periodically, LIC launches "Special Revival Campaigns." During these drives, LIC may offer to reimburse a portion of the medical costs or offer concessions on the late fee (interest). If you are reviving during a campaign, check with your branch if you are eligible for any cost waivers in the ANANDA system.

40. Can LIC refuse revival of a policy?

The Power to Reject

Yes, LIC has the absolute legal right to refuse the revival of a lapsed policy. Many policyholders mistakenly believe that if they pay the arrears and the interest, LIC must accept it. However, under insurance law, a revival is considered a "New Proposal" or a fresh invitation to offer a contract.

Why Rejections Happen: If the DGH or Medical Reports reveal that the policyholder has developed a life-threatening illness (like advanced cancer, kidney failure, or severe heart disease) during the lapse period, LIC’s underwriters may decide that the risk is no longer "Insurable" at the original premium rates. LIC also monitors for "Anti-Selection," where a person who is terminally ill tries to revive a long-lapsed policy just to get a death claim for their family. This would be unfair to the other policyholders in the Life Fund.

What Happens if Refused: If the revival is rejected, your policy remains in its previous state. If it had acquired a "Paid-up" value, that value is safe and will be paid at maturity. If it had no value (lapsed within 2 years), you lose the premiums already paid. LIC will refund any Arrears or Interest you deposited for the revival attempt, but they will not revive the risk cover. This is why it is critical to pay premiums on time—once a policy lapses, your Future Health becomes a deciding factor in whether you can ever get that insurance back.

41. When does an LIC policy acquire surrender value?

The Liquidity Milestone

An LIC policy is a long-term financial contract. It does not possess "Liquidity" or cash value from day one because the initial premiums are primarily used to cover the cost of high-risk insurance (Mortality Charges) and administrative setups. The policy must "ripen" through a mandatory gestation period before any portion of the premium is accessible for withdrawal.

The 2-Year Rule: For almost all traditional plans issued after 2014, the policy acquires a Surrender Value only after at least 2 full years' premiums have been paid. For certain older plans or high-tenure policies, this requirement might be 3 years. Until this milestone is reconciled in the ANANDA backend, the policy has zero cash value.

Online Process: You can check your eligibility by logging into the Customer Portal. If the "Surrender Value" or "Loan" buttons are active, your policy has crossed the milestone. Offline Process: You can visit the branch and request a "Status Report." If the status shows "In-force" or "Paid-up" with 2+ years of payments, the branch manager can generate an official Surrender Value Quote. Surrendering before this period results in total loss of premiums.

42. What is Guaranteed Surrender Value (GSV)?

The Legal Minimum

Guaranteed Surrender Value (GSV) is the absolute minimum amount that LIC is legally bound to pay you if you exit the policy prematurely. This value is protected from market fluctuations and the performance of LIC’s investment fund, serving as a "financial floor" for your policy's worth.

Calculation Formula: GSV is calculated as [Total Premiums Paid (excluding riders/taxes) x GSV Factor]. Additionally, any vested bonuses are added after being multiplied by a "Bonus Surrender Factor." These GSV factors are clearly printed in your policy bond and increase as the policy gets older (e.g., 30% after 2 years, rising to 90% near maturity).

Online Access: You can view your GSV on the LIC Customer Portal under the "Policy Status" or "Tools" menu. Offline Process: If you need a certified GSV certificate for a bank or legal purpose, you must submit a written request at the servicing branch. The ANANDA system will generate a precise calculation based on the exact date of surrender. While GSV is guaranteed, it is almost always lower than the "Special Surrender Value" offered by LIC during long-term policy runs.

43. What is Special Surrender Value (SSV)?

Market-Linked Actuarial Value

The Special Surrender Value (SSV) is a higher, non-guaranteed payout that LIC offers based on the actuarial performance of the Life Fund. While GSV is a conservative legal minimum, SSV represents the "True Market Value" of your policy at the time of exit.

Formula Logic: SSV is calculated by taking the (Reduced Paid-Up Sum Assured + Total Vested Bonuses) and multiplying it by an SSV Factor. These factors are reviewed periodically by LIC’s actuarial department and vary based on your age and the remaining time until maturity.

Online/Offline Check: LIC’s ANANDA system automatically calculates both GSV and SSV and always pays the Higher of the Two to the customer. You can check the current SSV quote online through the portal's "Surrender Quote" tool. Offline Process: You can obtain a detailed SSV breakdown from the branch counter. It is important to note that surrendering early usually makes the SSV much lower than the premiums paid; hence, SSV is only financially viable if the policy has run for at least 7–10 years.

44. How is Surrender Value calculated in LIC?

The Dual-Check Method

Surrender value calculation is a rigorous process within the ANANDA system designed to ensure fairness for the policyholder. LIC does not simply give a random refund; it performs two distinct mathematical audits before finalizing the payout.

The Procedure (Online/Offline): First, the system calculates the GSV (based on total premiums paid). Second, it calculates the SSV (based on the paid-up value and bonuses). The final amount credited to the user is Higher of (GSV or SSV). Any outstanding policy loans or unpaid interest are automatically deducted from this figure before the final credit.

Online/Offline Process: You can initiate a "Surrender Quote" request on the portal to see the math. For the actual payout, the **Offline process** is mandatory: you must surrender the Original Policy Bond and sign Form No. 5074 (Discharge Voucher) at the branch. Once the "Surrender Discharge" is updated in the system, the risk cover ends permanently. You cannot "undo" a surrender or revive a policy once the surrender value has been accepted by you.

45. What is Paid-Up Value in LIC?

The Mid-way Alternative

Paid-Up Status is a powerful feature that allows a policyholder to stop paying premiums without completely losing their insurance benefits or their accumulated savings. It is the "middle ground" between surrendering the policy and continuing the full premium payments.

Mechanism: If you have paid for at least 2 years and then stop, the policy doesn't "Lapse." Instead, the Sum Assured is scaled down to a smaller amount (pro-rata). This reduced amount remains active as a life cover until the original maturity date. You do not need to pay any further premiums.

Online/Offline: You can view your "Paid-Up Sum Assured" on the Customer Portal. It will show a reduced figure. Offline: You don't need to apply for this; it happens automatically in ANANDA if premiums are not paid and the grace period expires. However, if you want a physical endorsement of this status, you must submit the bond at the branch. This is far better than surrendering, as it keeps some level of risk cover alive and pays out the full accrued bonuses at the end of the term.

46. How is Paid-Up Sum Assured calculated?

The Pro-Rata Formula

The calculation of the **Paid-Up Sum Assured** is based on a strictly mathematical "Pro-Rata" formula. This ensures that the insurance cover you get is perfectly proportional to the amount of money you actually contributed to the Life Fund.

The Formula:
[Paid-up SA] = [Original SA] x (Total Number of Premiums Paid / Total Number of Premiums Payable). For example, if your SA is ₹10 Lakhs for 20 years, and you stop after 10 years, your new SA becomes ₹5 Lakhs. Any bonuses earned before the policy became paid-up remain attached to this ₹5 Lakhs in full.

Online/Offline Access: You can use the "Surrender & Paid-up" calculator on the LIC Portal to see this math in action. Offline: A "Status Report" from the branch counter will show the exact "Reduced Sum Assured." This calculation is handled centrally by ANANDA, meaning it is identical across all branches. This reduced SA is what LIC will pay in case of death or maturity. Future bonuses are usually not added, but the core amount is locked and guaranteed for the remainder of the policy term.

47. Which is better: Surrender, Paid-Up, or Loan?

Making the Right Choice

Choosing between Surrender, Paid-Up, and Loan depends entirely on your current financial goal. Each choice has a different impact on your long-term wealth and insurance protection, and the **ANANDA** system treats them with different priority levels.

Comparison (Online/Offline): 1. Surrender: Use this ONLY if you need immediate cash and never want the insurance again. It is the worst choice mathematically as you lose the "Cost of Insurance" already paid. 2. Paid-Up: Best if you can't pay premiums anymore but want to keep the money safe until maturity. 3. Policy Loan: This is the **smartest option** for temporary emergencies.

Decision Logic: With a loan, your full risk cover and full bonus accrual continue as if you paid normally. You can apply for a loan **Online** and get funds in 24 hours. Surrender and Paid-up processes are mostly **Offline** and involve a permanent reduction in your financial protection. LIC always advises taking a loan first; if you can't repay the loan, LIC will simply deduct it from the maturity later, keeping your risk cover alive in the meantime.

48. How can I take a loan against my LIC policy?

Instant Credit Facility

An LIC policy is a highly liquid asset for loan purposes. Since the loan is granted against your own "Surrender Value," there is no need for income proof, CIBIL scores, or third-party guarantors. The approval is essentially guaranteed by your policy's accrued value.

Online Process: This is the fastest method. Log in to the LIC Customer Portal, click on "Service Request," and select "Loan." If your Aadhaar is linked and bank details are verified, the ANANDA system will process the loan digitally. You don't need to visit the branch; the funds are credited via NEFT.

Offline Process: Visit the servicing branch and fill Form No. 500. You must submit the Original Policy Bond. LIC will keep the bond as collateral and return it only after the loan is repaid. They will endorse the loan details on the back of the bond. Note: You can pay back only the interest every 6 months and keep the principal as it is, or repay the principal in small parts. It is the most flexible loan in the Indian financial market.

49. How is Loan Eligibility calculated in LIC?

The 90% Rule

Loan eligibility is strictly tied to the **Surrender Value** of the policy at the time of application, not the Sum Assured. LIC ensures that the loan amount never exceeds the actual cash value of the policy to protect the corporation's Life Fund.

Calculations (Online/Offline): 1. In-Force Policies: You can get a loan of up to 90% of the current Surrender Value. 2. Paid-Up Policies: The limit is slightly lower, usually 85% of the Surrender Value, because the policy is no longer receiving new premium inflows.

ANANDA Logic: When you apply online or at the counter, the system fetches the current SV, applies the percentage, and deducts any existing loan or interest. The remaining balance is your "Maximum Loan Eligibility." You can choose to take a smaller amount than the maximum. Example: If your SV is ₹2 Lakhs, you can instantly get ₹1.8 Lakhs. This amount is updated daily in the system as your bonuses grow, meaning your loan eligibility increases every single day your policy stays active.

50. What is the interest rate on LIC policy loans?

Simple vs. Compounded Interest

The interest rate on LIC policy loans is typically much lower than personal loans or credit cards. The rate is set by LIC’s central office and is usually linked to the prevailing market rates (currently between 9% and 10.5% p.a. for most plans).

Payment Cycle (Online/Offline): Interest is calculated on a Half-Yearly basis. You will receive a "Loan Interest Intimation" every six months. You can pay this interest **Online** via the portal or at any LIC cash counter. If you pay the interest on time, the principal remains constant.

The Compounding Rule: If you fail to pay the interest by the due date, the ANANDA system will automatically add the unpaid interest to your **Principal Loan Amount**. This is called compounding. Foreclosure Risk: If the total (Principal + Unpaid Interest) grows so large that it equals or exceeds the Surrender Value of the policy, LIC has the legal right to "Foreclose" (terminate) the policy to recover the debt. Therefore, it is critical to pay the interest even if you cannot pay the principal.

51. What is the procedure for maturity claim in LIC?

The Harvest Stage

A **Maturity Claim** is the final payout of an insurance contract. It occurs when the policyholder survives the entire term of the policy. At this stage, LIC pays out the Sum Assured plus all the bonuses accumulated over 15, 20, or 25 years.

Online/Offline Initiation: LIC’s ANANDA system identifies maturity cases 2–3 months in advance and sends a "Maturity Intimation" letter to your registered address. You can also see this "Maturity Due" status on the portal. However, the settlement process is primarily **Offline**. You must submit the Original Policy Bond and the signed **Discharge Voucher (Form No. 3806)** at the branch.

Validation: You must affix a **₹1 Revenue Stamp** on the voucher and sign across it. LIC will also verify your latest bank details. Once the branch "reconciles" the documents, the system triggers the payment. The amount is usually credited on the exact date of maturity. If you don't submit documents on time, the money is kept in a "Suspense Account" and no interest is paid for the delay, so early submission (30 days before maturity) is advised.

52. Is maturity claim amount paid automatically?

Prerequisites for Speed

While LIC has the goal of "On-time Payouts," the maturity claim is not 100% automatic for everyone. It requires a "Clean Record" in the **ANANDA** system. For the money to reach your account exactly on the maturity date without you following up, two conditions must be met.

The Conditions (Online/Offline): 1. NEFT Mandate: Your bank details must be fully verified and "KYC-Compliant" in the digital records. 2. Bond Submission: The physical policy bond must have reached the branch at least 15 days before the maturity date. If these are in place, the branch will pre-approve the payout.

The Payout: On the morning of your maturity date, the system triggers a direct bank transfer. If your record is "Incomplete" (e.g., bank details missing), the ANANDA system will block the payout. LIC will send you a reminder, but the money will stay with LIC. Practical Tip: Check your "Bank Details" status on the portal 6 months before maturity. If it's missing, submit Form 3801 immediately to ensure your maturity happens automatically.

53. What documents are required for maturity claim?

Maturity Checklist

To settle a maturity claim, LIC requires a specific set of legal and financial documents to satisfy the ANANDA system's audit requirements. These are collectively called the "Maturity Kit." Errors in these documents are the number one cause of payment delays.

The Checklist (Offline): 1. Original Policy Bond: The most important document. If you lost it, you must complete the "Duplicate Bond" process first. 2. Discharge Voucher (Form 3806): This is your legal acknowledgment that you have received the money. It must be signed and witnessed. 3. Cancelled Cheque: It must clearly show your name and the IFSC code. 4. ₹1 Revenue Stamp: Affixed on the 3806 form; you must sign across the stamp and the paper.

Verification: Your signature on the Form 3806 must match the signature you provided 20 years ago in the Original Proposal. If there is a major change in signature, LIC will ask for an additional "Signature Attestation" from your bank. You can download these forms **Online** from the "Downloads" section of the LIC website, but you must submit the signed physical copies to the branch. Once submitted, the branch scans them into the system for final payout approval.

54. What is survival benefit in money back policies?

Periodic Liquidity

Survival Benefits (SB) are unique to "Money-Back" plans. Unlike Endowment plans where you get money only at the end, Money-Back plans return a portion of the Sum Assured to you at fixed intervals (e.g., every 4 or 5 years). This provides liquidity for milestones like a child's school admission or home renovation.

The Key Logic: Receiving an SB does **not** reduce your life insurance cover. For example, if you have a ₹10 Lakh policy and receive ₹2 Lakhs as your first SB, your life cover remains ₹10 Lakhs plus bonuses. LIC treats these as "Interim Payouts."

Online/Offline Tracking: You can check your upcoming SB dates on the LIC Portal under "Policy Schedule." The ANANDA system automatically tracks these dates. You don't need to apply for an SB; it is paid automatically. However, there is a **Condition**: the policy must be "In-force." If the policy is lapsed, the SB is withheld. Once you revive the policy, the "Held SB" is released and credited to your account along with the revival confirmation. This makes SB a great indicator of your policy's health status.

55. How are survival benefits paid by LIC?

The Seamless Payout

LIC has made the payment of **Survival Benefits** almost entirely friction-less. Unlike the final maturity claim, you do not need to visit the branch or submit your policy bond to receive an SB payout. The process is handled entirely by the ANANDA central server.

The Payout Process (Online): On the due date, the system checks if the policy is "In-force" and if a verified NEFT mandate exists. If yes, the money is pushed to your bank account via direct credit. You will receive an SMS and an email confirmation. This is why keeping your bank details updated is so critical for Money-Back policyholders.

Failure Scenarios (Offline): If the NEFT fails (due to wrong IFSC or closed account), the money reverts to LIC. You will then have to visit the branch and submit a Fresh NEFT Mandate (Form 3801). The system will then "re-trigger" the SB payment. Note that if you have an outstanding policy loan, LIC will first adjust the Unpaid Loan Interest from the SB amount and pay you only the balance. This ensures that your loan doesn't go into "Foreclosure" status.

56. What is the procedure for death claim settlement?

Claiming the Protection

The **Death Claim** is the ultimate purpose of an insurance contract. LIC treats death claims with high priority, as the company's reputation depends on its "Claim Settlement Ratio." The process is designed to be empathetic but rigorous to prevent fraud against the Life Fund.

Step 1 (Offline): The nominee must visit the servicing branch to **Intimate the Death**. You need to provide the **Original Death Certificate**. LIC will then provide a "Claim Kit" containing various forms (Form A, B, C, etc.). Online Status: You can intimate the death through the LIC website, but the actual submission of physical forms and the original bond is mandatory at the branch.

Verification: The ANANDA system checks if the policy was "In-force" at the time of death. If death occurred within the grace period, the claim is still paid. LIC verifies the nominee's KYC and bank details. Once all documents are scrutinized and found "In-order," the claim is approved. The final payout includes the Sum Assured + Accrued Bonuses + FAB. The entire process for a non-investigative claim usually takes 15 days from the date of final document submission.

57. What documents are required for death claim?

The Documentary Proof

To settle a death claim, LIC requires a "Chain of Evidence" that proves the death of the life assured and the identity of the claimant. Any missing link in this chain will stop the ANANDA system from authorizing the payout. The requirements are stricter for "Early Claims" (death within 3 years).

The Kit (Offline): 1. Original Death Certificate: Issued by the municipal authority. LIC will verify this with the issuer. 2. Original Policy Bond: Essential to prove title. 3. Claim Form A: Nominee's formal statement. 4. Claim Form B & C: To be filled by the doctor who attended the deceased and the hospital. 5. Burial/Cremation Certificate: To prove the disposal of the body.

Additional Docs: For accidental death, an FIR and Post-Mortem Report are mandatory to trigger the "Accident Benefit Rider." The nominee must also submit their **Cancelled Cheque** and KYC. While you can download these forms **Online**, they must be submitted physically. The branch scans these into the digital file for the Zonal or Divisional Office to audit. If the nominee is a minor, the Appointee's documents become mandatory.

58. What is early death claim and how is it treated?

High-Scrutiny Claims

An **Early Death Claim** is a claim where the policyholder passes away within 3 years of the policy commencement or the last revival. LIC treats these with extra caution to prevent "Moral Hazard"—cases where someone knowingly takes a policy while hiding a terminal illness.

The Treatment (Offline): For early claims, a Field Investigation is mandatory. LIC officials may visit the deceased's workplace, hospitals, and neighbors to verify the medical history. They check if any "Material Fact" (like a pre-existing heart condition) was hidden in the proposal form. Under Section 45 of the Insurance Act, LIC has the power to question the claim on grounds of fraud within this 3-year window.

System Logic: The ANANDA system automatically flags these cases. The payout is blocked until the "Investigation Report" is uploaded and cleared by the Divisional Manager. Result: If the investigation proves the death was natural and all disclosures were true, the claim is paid with full interest for the time taken. If fraud is found, the claim is **Repudiated** (rejected), and premiums may be forfeited. After 3 years, the policy becomes "Indisputable," and LIC generally cannot reject the claim unless they prove extreme fraud.

59. How much time does LIC take to settle a claim?

Turnaround Time (TAT)

LIC is bound by the **IRDAI Protection of Policyholders' Interests Regulations** regarding claim timelines. The ANANDA system has built-in timers that alert senior management if a claim is pending beyond the stipulated time. The corporation aims for the highest "Claim Settlement Ratio" in the industry.

Standard Timelines (Online/Offline): 1. Normal Claims: Once all complete documents are submitted, the goal is to settle within 15 days. 2. Maturity Claims: These are settled on the **Actual Due Date**. 3. Investigated Claims: If the claim requires field work, LIC has up to 90 days to finalize the decision.

The Penalty Rule: If LIC delays the payment beyond 30 days of receiving all "last-mile" documents, and the delay is not due to the claimant, LIC must pay Interest at a rate that is 2% above the prevailing bank rate. You can track your "Claim Progress" **Online** through the portal using your "Claim Reference Number." Most delays happen because the nominee hasn't submitted a cancelled cheque or the signature didn't match; ensuring "In-order" paperwork is the best way to speed up the TAT.

60. Can LIC reject a claim and on what grounds?

The Grounds for Repudiation

While LIC pays more than 99% of its claims, it is legally and ethically bound to **Repudiate (Reject)** fraudulent claims to protect the "Life Fund" belonging to millions of honest policyholders. A rejection is never arbitrary; it must be backed by documentary evidence that would stand up in a Consumer Court.

Main Grounds (Offline/Legal): 1. Suppression of Material Facts: Hiding a serious illness (like Kidney disease or Cancer) that existed at the time of taking the policy. 2. Fraud: Identity theft or staging a fake death. 3. Lapse: If the death occurred when the policy was not "In-force" and beyond the grace period. 4. Suicide Clause: Death by suicide within 12 months of policy start or revival is excluded. In such cases, LIC only refunds 80% of premiums paid, not the Sum Assured.

Dispute Resolution: If a claim is rejected, LIC sends a "Repudiation Letter" explaining the reasons. The nominee can then appeal to the **Zonal Claims Dispute Redressal Committee (ZCDRC)**. If still unsatisfied, the claimant can approach the **Insurance Ombudsman** or a Consumer Forum. Every rejection is recorded in the ANANDA system and reported to the IRDAI. Honest disclosure at the proposal stage is the only way to ensure 100% claim safety.

61. What is Simple Reversionary Bonus (SRB) in LIC?

Profit Sharing (SRB)

Simple Reversionary Bonus (SRB) is the primary method through which LIC distributes its annual valuation surplus to Participating (With-Profit) policyholders. It is a form of wealth creation that grows your policy's value over time. It is termed "Simple" because it is calculated as a percentage of the Basic Sum Assured only, and does not earn "interest on interest" like a compound bonus would.

Online Tracking: Policyholders can view their total accumulated (vested) bonus by logging into the LIC Customer Portal. Under the "Policy Status" or "Bonus Information" tab, the system pulls real-time data from the ANANDA backend to show exactly how much bonus has been added to the policy since inception.

Offline Process & Rules: Every year after the March 31st valuation, LIC declares the bonus rates (usually expressed as Rs. X per Rs. 1,000 Sum Assured). Once added, the bonus becomes "Vested," meaning it is a permanent legal asset of the policyholder and LIC cannot take it back. However, SRB is not liquid; it is paid as a lump sum only during the final Maturity or Death Claim. If the policy is surrendered early, the bonus is paid at a reduced "Surrender Value Factor" rate. To receive the full declared SRB, the policy must be In-force on the date of declaration.

62. What is Final Additional Bonus (FAB)?

The Loyalty Reward

Final Additional Bonus (FAB) is a one-time terminal bonus paid by LIC as a reward for long-term "Persistency." While the annual SRB rewards you every year, the FAB is designed to reward policyholders who stay with the corporation until the very end of the contract or whose policies result in a death claim after many years.

Eligibility & Rules: FAB is typically only payable if the policy has run for a minimum duration, usually 15 years or more. The rate of FAB is significantly higher for larger Sum Assured Slabs (e.g., a policy of 10 Lakhs gets a much higher FAB rate than a policy of 1 Lakh). Crucially, FAB is **never paid on early surrender**. If you exit the policy even one year before maturity, you forfeit the entire FAB.

Online/Offline Verification: You can use the "Maturity Calculators" available on the LIC Portal to estimate your FAB based on current declaration trends. Offline: When LIC sends the "Maturity Intimation Letter" 2 months before the due date, the ANANDA system performs a final audit and specifies the exact FAB amount payable. It is added to the Sum Assured and SRB to form the final "Settlement Value." FAB rates are reviewed annually and depend on the surplus generated by LIC’s long-term investments.

63. When is Bonus added to an LIC policy?

Bonus Timing

The addition of a bonus to an LIC policy follows a strictly regulated Annual Valuation Cycle. It is not added daily, monthly, or on your policy anniversary. Instead, it depends on the fiscal health of the corporation as determined on the last day of the Indian financial year.

The Timeline: 1. March 31st: LIC closes its books and the Actuary begins evaluating the "Life Fund" surplus. 2. August/September: After the audit is approved by the IRDAI, LIC officially declares the bonus rates for that year. 3. Retrospective Credit: Even though the declaration happens in August, the bonus is credited "backwards" for the year that ended on March 31st.

Online/Offline Status: On the Customer Portal, you will see the new bonus reflected in your "Accumulated Bonus" total only after the system-wide update in the ANANDA database (usually by October). Offline Requirement: To receive the bonus for a particular year, the policy must be "In-force" (all premiums paid) on the date the bonus is declared. If your policy lapses in May, it will not receive the bonus declared in August for that year, even if you paid for the full previous year. This rule ensures that only participating members who contribute to the risk pool share in the profits.

64. How is Bonus calculated in LIC policies?

The Mathematical Formula

LIC uses a transparent, Sum-Assured-linked formula to calculate the bonus. Unlike some private insurers who calculate bonuses based on the "Premium Paid," LIC calculates the bonus on the **Basic Sum Assured**. This is a massive advantage for the policyholder because the Sum Assured is usually 10 to 20 times the annual premium.

The Formula (Offline/Online):
[Annual Bonus = (Basic Sum Assured / 1000) x Declared Rate].
For example, if you have a policy with a Sum Assured of ₹10,00,000 and LIC declares a bonus rate of ₹45, the calculation is: (1,00,00,00 / 1000) * 45 = ₹45,000 for that year.

System Automation: The ANANDA software automatically performs this calculation for millions of policies simultaneously. You can see this math in action on the LIC Portal by checking your "Bonus History." Over a 20-year term, if the rate remains consistent, a ₹10 Lakh policy would accumulate ₹9 Lakhs in bonuses alone, making the total maturity payout ₹19 Lakhs (plus FAB). This math is the reason why LIC policies are considered "Wealth Generators" rather than just insurance covers. It is important to note that Rider Sum Assured is never included in bonus calculations.

65. Do Paid-Up policies get Bonus?

Bonus on Reduced Value

One of the most unique and fair features of LIC’s underwriting rules is the treatment of Paid-Up Policies. When you stop paying premiums after the mandatory 2-year period, the policy doesn't "Die"—it enters a "Reduced Paid-Up" state. Most people assume that bonus accrual stops here, but that is not true for traditional participating plans.

The Pro-Rata Rule (Offline/ANANDA): LIC continues to add bonuses to a Paid-up policy, but the calculation is based on the Reduced Sum Assured. For example, if your original SA was ₹10 Lakhs but it became Paid-Up at ₹4 Lakhs, the annual bonus formula will now use ₹4 Lakhs as the base.

Vesting of Past Bonuses: All bonuses that were added to your policy while it was In-force remain 100% safe and "Vested." They are not reduced. They will be paid in full along with the Paid-up SA at the end of the term. You can verify your "Reduced Bonus Accrual" by generating a Status Report from the portal or branch. This ensures that even if you face financial hardship and stop premiums, your past savings continue to grow, albeit at a slower pace, protecting your capital from complete stagnation.

66. What are Riders in LIC policies?

Customizing Protection

A Rider is a powerful contractual add-on that "rides" on your primary insurance policy. Think of the base policy as the "Engine" and riders as "Optional Accessories" that customize and strengthen your financial shield. Riders allow you to get specialized protection—like accidental cover or critical illness cover—at a fraction of the cost of a standalone policy.

Nature & Limitations (Offline/Online): A rider is not an independent contract. If the base policy lapses or is surrendered, the rider ends automatically. You pay a small additional premium for each rider you choose. Because riders utilize the same administrative setup as the main policy, LIC can offer them at extremely competitive rates.

Usage: You can select riders during the Proposal Stage (Offline via Form 300 or Online via the website). The ANANDA system limits the total rider Sum Assured so that it usually does not exceed the Base Sum Assured. For example, if your base life cover is ₹5 Lakhs, you can typically take an Accidental Rider of up to ₹5 Lakhs, effectively creating a ₹10 Lakh cover for accidents. Riders are the most cost-effective way to handle "High-Impact, Low-Probability" events like permanent disability or specific dread diseases.

67. What types of Riders does LIC offer?

The Rider Menu

LIC provides a comprehensive suite of riders designed to meet different risk profiles. These riders are integrated into the ANANDA system and can be mixed and matched depending on the plan chosen.

Core Riders Explained: 1. ADDB (Accidental Death & Disability Benefit): The most popular rider. It pays Double the SA in case of accidental death and provides a monthly income + premium waiver in case of permanent disability. 2. Term Assurance Rider: This increases your natural death cover significantly at a very low cost, perfect for young breadwinners. 3. Critical Illness Rider: Provides a lump sum payout if you are diagnosed with one of the 15 pre-defined diseases (like Cancer or Stroke). 4. PWB (Premium Waiver Benefit): Essential for child plans (like Jeevan Tarun). If the parent dies, all future premiums for the child's policy are waived by LIC, but the child still gets all maturity benefits.

Online/Offline Availability: You can compare the costs of these riders using the LIC Premium Calculator online. When filling the proposal form offline, you must specifically tick the boxes for these riders and provide additional health info if choosing the Critical Illness option. Each rider has its own Maximum Ceiling (e.g., ₹1 Crore for ADDB across all policies), which the system validates before issuing the bond.

68. Can Riders be added after policy issuance?

Timing is Everything

A common request from policyholders is to add a rider (like the Accident or Critical Illness rider) midway through the policy term. However, in the vast majority of LIC’s traditional plans, the answer is Strictly No. Riders must be opted for at the **Inception** of the policy (at the time of the initial proposal).

The Logic (Underwriting): This rule exists to prevent "Anti-Selection." For example, LIC does not want a person to add an Accidental Rider only after they have taken up a high-risk job, or a Critical Illness rider after they start feeling unwell. The risk must be assessed and priced when the life is "Fresh."

Exceptions (Offline): Some specific new-age plans allow the addition of the "Accident Benefit" rider on a policy anniversary, but this is rare and requires a Physical Application at the branch and fresh health underwriting. You cannot add riders via the Customer Portal post-issuance. Practical Tip: Always review your rider needs with an agent before signing the proposal form. If you miss adding a rider, your only option is usually to buy a new "Base Policy" that includes that specific rider, which will be more expensive due to your increased age.

69. Do Riders have maturity value?

Pure Protection Cost

It is crucial to understand that Riders are Pure Protection tools, not investment components. While your base Endowment or Money Back policy accumulates a "Cash Value" (Bonus and SA) that you get back at the end, the money you pay for a rider is a "Fee" for carrying a specific risk.

The Math (Offline/Online): If you pay ₹1,000 extra per year for an Accidental Rider for 20 years, and you do not meet with an accident, you get ₹0 back from that rider at maturity. The ₹20,000 you paid was the price for being covered for ₹10 Lakhs every single day of those 20 years.

Comparison: Riders work exactly like Car Insurance or Health Insurance. You pay the premium to ensure that if something happens, the payout is massive. If nothing happens, you have enjoyed the "Peace of Mind." The ANANDA system calculates your maturity payout by adding the Base SA + SRB + FAB; the rider premiums are excluded from this calculation. This is why rider premiums are so low—you are only paying for the risk, not for a savings return. Always treat rider costs as a necessary expense for a robust financial safety net.

70. How are Rider claims settled?

Concurrent Settlement

Rider claims are not settled automatically just because a base claim is filed. They require a separate Eligibility Audit within the ANANDA system. While the base death claim is paid for any reason (except suicide in the first year), a rider claim (like ADDB) requires proof that the death met the rider’s specific definition of an "Accident."

Offline Documentation (Critical): For an Accidental Rider claim, the nominee must provide: 1. FIR (First Information Report) from the police. 2. Post-Mortem Report confirming the cause of death. 3. Newspaper Cuttings (if available). If the claim is for "Permanent Disability," LIC will appoint a medical board to verify that the disability is 100% and irreversible.

Payout Logic: Once approved, the rider amount is paid In addition to the base sum. If you have a ₹5 Lakh base policy with a ₹5 Lakh ADDB rider, and die in a bike accident, your family gets ₹10 Lakhs + Bonuses. If you die of a heart attack, they only get ₹5 Lakhs + Bonuses. You can track the "Rider Settlement Status" **Online** through the portal. The transparency of this dual-layer payout is a cornerstone of LIC’s trust, ensuring that families get extra support when a sudden, unforeseen tragedy strikes.

71. What is medical underwriting in LIC?

The Science of Risk

Medical Underwriting is the specialized process through which LIC assesses the "Quality of Risk" of a proposer. It is the gatekeeping mechanism that ensures the Life Fund remains solvent by not accepting lives that are too high-risk without proper pricing (loading). It is based on the Actuarial Principle that everyone should pay a premium proportionate to their likelihood of filing a claim.

The Three Pillars (Offline/System): Underwriters in the ANANDA backend look at: 1. Medical History: Existing conditions like Diabetes or Hypertension. 2. Physicality: Height, Weight, and BMI (to check for obesity-related risks). 3. Family Longevity: How long your parents and siblings have lived.

Decision Matrix: After the audit, the underwriter makes a choice: **Accept at Ordinary Rates** (Healthy), **Accept with Loading** (Minor issues), **Postpone** (Temporary illness), or **Decline** (Severe risk). You can see your "Underwriting Status" **Online** during the proposal phase. This process protects existing healthy policyholders from having to subsidize the claims of high-risk individuals, maintaining the financial integrity of the corporation for everyone.

72. What is Tele-MER in LIC?

The Digital Interview

Tele-MER (Telephonic Medical Examination Report) is a modern, high-tech alternative to the traditional physical medical exam. It was introduced to speed up the issuance of policies and provide convenience to customers in remote locations or with busy schedules. It acts as a recorded legal interview regarding your health.

How it Works (Online): Once you submit a proposal for a moderate Sum Assured, an LIC-authorized doctor or a medical professional will call your registered mobile number. The call is conducted through a Secure Recording Line. You will be asked a series of structured questions: Do you smoke? Have you had surgery? What is your exact height/weight?

Legal Standing: Your verbal answers are Legally Binding. If you lie during a Tele-MER, it is treated the same as lying on a paper form, and can lead to claim rejection. The audio file is encrypted and uploaded directly to the ANANDA system for the underwriter to review. This method is primarily used for "Non-Medical" categories where the Sum Assured is higher than the standard limit but not high enough to require a full hospital visit. It represents LIC's shift toward Contactless Underwriting.

73. What is Video-MER in LIC?

Visual Risk Assessment

Video-MER is the highest level of digital medical assessment. While Tele-MER is just audio, Video-MER allows the doctor to visually verify the proposer. This is used for high-value policies to prevent "Proxy Underwriting"—a type of fraud where a healthy person pretends to be the ill policyholder during a phone call or hospital visit.

The Observation (Online): During the live video link, the doctor observes the proposer's Gait (walking style), skin condition, and breath. They will ask you to show your Original Photo ID (Aadhaar/PAN) to the camera. This creates a "Digital Proof of Life" and "Proof of Health" that is impossible to forge.

Integration: The ANANDA system sends a secure, one-time link via SMS. You do not need to download an app; it works through your smartphone browser. The entire interaction is timestamped and geotagged. Once the video call ends, the doctor submits a "Visual Health Score." This method has revolutionized NRI Underwriting, allowing Indians living abroad to buy high-value policies without flying back to India for medical tests. It balances extreme convenience with the highest level of security for the corporation.

74. What are third-party MSPs in LIC medicals?

Authorized Medical Partners

To ensure standardized and tamper-proof medical reports, LIC has empanelled large, accredited diagnostic chains known as MSPs (Medical Service Providers). These include names like Thyrocare, Healthians, and other ISO-certified laboratories. By using third-party MSPs, LIC removes the human element of "Local Influence" from the medical reporting process.

Data Integrity (Online): The most important feature of the MSP system is the Direct Digital Upload. When you give a blood or urine sample, the lab processes it and uploads the results directly to the ANANDA portal via an API (Application Programming Interface). Neither the policyholder nor the insurance agent ever sees the physical report before it reaches the underwriter. This eliminates the possibility of report tampering or forging.

Home Convenience (Offline): Most MSPs offer Home Collection. A technician will visit your home at a scheduled time (usually morning for fasting blood tests). You can book these appointments **Online** through the link provided by LIC. This has made the medical part of buying insurance significantly less stressful. Using corporate MSPs also ensures that the tests are conducted using High-Precision Equipment, providing the underwriter with accurate data for a fair decision.

75. When does LIC insist on medical examination?

Identifying High-Risk Proposals

Not every person who buys an LIC policy needs to see a doctor. LIC uses a sophisticated "Grid" in the ANANDA system to decide when to insist on a medical examination. This grid balances the cost of medical testing against the risk of the policy payout.

The Triggers (Offline/System): 1. SUC (Sum Under Consideration): If the total insurance cover you are seeking (including policies taken in the last 2 years) exceeds certain limits (e.g., ₹20 Lakhs for a 35-year-old), medicals are triggered. 2. Age Factor: Proposers above a certain age (typically 45, 50, or 55) are automatically required to undergo tests like ECG, FBS, and HbA1c, regardless of the Sum Assured, because lifestyle diseases are statistically more common in these age groups. 3. Adverse Declaration: If you mention a past surgery or chronic medicine in the proposal form, the system will call for a targeted test.

Online Notification: You will find out if medicals are required the moment the proposal is entered into the system. A "Requirement Letter" is generated. 4. Revivals: If you are reviving a policy after a long lapse, medicals are almost always mandatory to ensure you haven't developed a terminal illness while the policy was inactive. This disciplined approach ensures that the Life Fund is not exposed to "Hidden Risks."

76. Can LIC issue policy without medical tests?

Non-Medical Schemes

Yes, LIC has extensive Non-Medical (NM) categories designed for "Clean" proposals. These schemes allow policies to be issued based solely on the health declaration in the proposal form, without any doctor's visit or lab test. This is the fastest way to get insured.

The Categories (Offline/Online): 1. NM Special: For employees of the Government, PSUs, Banks, and large Reputed Corporates. These individuals are considered lower risk because they usually undergo regular health checkups for their jobs. Limits are higher here (up to ₹50 Lakhs SA for young ages). 2. NM Professional: For Doctors, CAs, Engineers, and MBAs who have a stable income and a disciplined lifestyle. 3. NM General: For the general public, including self-employed individuals, with lower SA limits.

Rules: To qualify for a non-medical policy, the proposer must be in a "Standard Profession" (not hazardous) and must not have a history of major illness. The ANANDA system automatically validates these criteria. If you fall within the NM limits, your policy is "Fast-Tracked." However, even in NM cases, LIC reserves the right to call for a random medical checkup if the underwriter spots a red flag in the proposal. NM schemes rely on the **Principle of Trust**—if you hide a disease in an NM policy, the claim can still be rejected later under Section 45.

77. What if adverse medical findings are reported?

Handling Health Red Flags

If your medical reports (FMR, Blood tests, or ECG) show an abnormality, such as high blood sugar or a borderline heart rhythm, it does not necessarily mean your policy will be rejected. LIC’s underwriting department uses a Numerical Rating System to quantify the increased risk and find a way to still provide you with insurance.

The Outcomes (Offline/Online): 1. Loading (Extra Premium): If the risk is manageable (e.g., controlled Hypertension), LIC will ask for an "Extra Premium." This extra money is kept in a separate reserve to cover the statistically higher chance of a claim. 2. Postponement: If you have a temporary condition (e.g., you just had a minor surgery or an acute infection), the underwriter may ask you to wait for 6 to 12 months before reapplying. 3. Decline: Only in cases of severe, life-threatening, or unmanageable conditions (like Stage 4 Cancer or end-stage organ failure) will LIC decline the risk to protect the Life Fund.

Communication: You will receive a formal "Letter of Offer" via email or post. The ANANDA system will pause the proposal until you accept the loading and pay the difference. This process is transparent, and you have the right to refuse the offer and take a full refund of your deposit. Adverse findings are a "Health Alert" for the customer, often helping them detect lifestyle diseases they weren't aware of.

78. Can LIC load premium based on medical report?

The Concept of 'Loading'

Loading is the technical term for the additional premium charged to a policyholder who has a higher-than-average risk profile. It is the core of **Equitable Underwriting**. It ensures that a 30-year-old marathon runner and a 30-year-old chronic smoker with high BP do not pay the same price for the same insurance benefit.

Types of Loading (System Logic): 1. EML (Extra Mortality Loading): This is the most common, based on health issues like BMI or Diabetes. 2. Occupational Loading: If you work in a dangerous job (like a deep-sea diver or an explosives handler). 3. Moral Hazard Loading: Very rare, used if there are doubts about the financial background of the proposer.

Acceptance Process (Offline/Online): If a loading is applied, the ANANDA system generates an "Amendment Slip." You must sign this slip and pay the extra premium amount. Practical Impact: Loading does not affect your bonus or Sum Assured; it only affects the cost you pay every year. For example, if your standard premium is ₹10,000 and LIC applies a 20% loading, you will pay ₹12,000. Many policyholders find it beneficial to accept the loading because it is still cheaper than being uninsured. If you refuse the loading, your proposal is cancelled, and LIC will refund your initial deposit (after deducting medical costs).

79. What is the role of LIC panel doctor?

The Medical Gatekeeper

A Panel Doctor is an independent medical professional authorized and empanelled by LIC to conduct physical examinations of proposers. They are the "Eyes and Ears" of the Underwriting department. While an agent sells the policy, the doctor verifies if the life being sold is actually as healthy as described in the form.

Responsibilities (Offline): 1. Identity Verification: The doctor must verify that the person sitting in their chamber is the same person whose photo is on the ID card. 2. Clinical Observation: They record objective findings: BP, Pulse, Breath sounds, and surgical scars. 3. Signature Witnessing: The doctor witnesses your signature on the Medical Examiner’s Report (MER), ensuring no one else signed for you.

Limitation: A panel doctor does not decide if a policy is accepted or rejected. They only record the "Clinical Facts." The decision is made by LIC’s own Underwriters who review the doctor's report in the ANANDA system. You can find the nearest panel doctor through the LIC Portal or by asking your branch. Reports from non-empanelled doctors are strictly invalid. This standardized process ensures that every proposer across India is examined using the same clinical rigor, preventing local bias or favoritism in the insurance process.

80. Can LIC cancel a policy for false medical disclosure?

The Penalty for Dishonesty

Yes. LIC has the legal authority to **Void** a contract and cancel a policy if it was obtained through "Fraudulent Suppression" of health facts. Insurance is a contract of Uberrima Fides (Utmost Good Faith). If you break that trust by hiding a serious medical condition to get lower rates or gain entry, the contract is legally dead from the beginning.

Section 45 of the Insurance Act: This is the most powerful law in the sector. It states that LIC cannot question a policy after 3 years for any reason *except* fraud. However, if fraud is proven (e.g., you were undergoing chemotherapy while writing "No illness" on the form), LIC can cancel the policy **at any time**, even 10 years later.

Consequences (Offline/Legal): 1. Claim Rejection: The family gets ₹0 in case of death. 2. Premium Forfeiture: Legally, LIC is allowed to Keep all the premiums you have paid as a penalty for fraud. This is much harsher than a simple surrender. 3. ANANDA Blacklisting: Your name may be flagged in the central database, making it difficult to buy insurance in the future. Moral: Always disclose every health condition, even minor ones. Paying a small "Loading" (Question 78) is a small price to pay for the absolute certainty that your family's claim will be paid without any legal hurdles.

81. What is the role of LIC agent after policy issuance?

The Service Facilitator

After the sale is closed, the LIC agent’s role shifts from a salesperson to a Service Facilitator. While the legal contract is strictly between the policyholder and the corporation, the agent acts as a vital "Knowledge Bridge" to ensure the contract remains healthy and the benefits are eventually realized.

Offline Support: A primary duty is Persistency Management—ensuring you don't forget premium dates to avoid lapse and late fees. Online/Legal Limitations: In the digital era, agents help customers navigate the LIC Customer Portal. However, it is vital to remember that the agent has No Statutory Authority. The ANANDA system requires the policyholder's own authentication (Signature or OTP) for all major changes. An agent’s role is to provide the "Correct Advice" so that the policyholder can make informed decisions about loans, surrenders, or riders.

82. Can an agent perform servicing on behalf of customer?

Limits of Representation

Offline Accountability: Never sign a blank servicing form and hand it to an agent. Forging a signature is a criminal offense and can lead to the Cancellation of Benefits. 84. Who can access the ANANDA system?

Controlled Data Access

Access to LIC’s core database (ANANDA) is strictly regulated to protect policyholder privacy and prevent financial malpractice. LIC follows a "Need-to-Know" protocol, where access levels are determined by the employee's official designation and role within the servicing cycle.

94. Can LIC policy be transferred after death?

95. Role of Legal Heir vs. Nominee?

Receiver vs. Owner

This is perhaps the most misunderstood part of insurance law in India. A nominee is a Receiver, whereas a legal heir is an Owner. LIC’s duty is simple: they are legally "Discharged" once they pay the registered Nominee. They do not check your family tree or your Will.

The Trustee Role (Offline/Legal): Legally, the nominee receives the money as a "Trustee." If you pass away leaving a wife (nominee) and three children (legal heirs), the wife is duty-bound to distribute the money among herself and the children according to the laws of succession. If a dispute arises, the heirs must approach a court for a Succession Certificate. LIC will not intervene in family fights; they only follow the ANANDA records.

Beneficial Nominee Rule: Under current law, if the nominee is a Spouse, Parent, or Child, they are treated as "Beneficial Nominees," meaning they are presumed to be the owners and can keep the money unless a Will states otherwise. If the nominee is a brother or a friend, they are strictly "Collectors" who must pass the money to the legal heirs. To ensure a smooth transfer of wealth, always ensure your nominee matches the person you want to actually own the money.

1. NACH (National Automated Clearing House): This is the modern standard managed by NPCI. It is highly reliable and centralized. Online Activation: You can often set up NACH via the portal or bank apps. It is the best mode for Monthly premiums. 2. ECS (Electronic Clearing Service): This is the "Legacy" version of NACH. It is being phased out as it is slower and requires manual intervention at the local branch. 3. SI (Standing Instruction): This is a direct agreement between you and your bank. Offline Setup: You submit a mandate at your bank branch. LIC prefers NACH because it is fully integrated with the ANANDA system, providing real-time success/failure reports. If you want a "Set it and Forget it" experience, always opt for NACH-M (Monthly) at the time of the proposal or via Form 3801 at the branch.

114. How can ECS/NACH be activated or cancelled for an LIC policy?

Managing Automated Mandates

Setting up or stopping an auto-debit requires a lead time of at least 30 days to ensure the banking "Handshake" is completed before the premium due date.

Online Mode: Currently, the LIC portal allows you to *view* your NACH status, but First-time Activation usually requires an Offline visit. You must submit Form No. 3801 (NACH Mandate) along with a **Cancelled Cheque** to your servicing branch. The branch scans this into ANANDA, which then sends the data to the **NPCI** hub for bank approval. Cancellation Mode: To stop an auto-debit, you must submit a written "De-registration Request" at the branch. You cannot simply stop it via the portal. If you don't cancel it at least 15 days before the due date, the system might still trigger a debit attempt. Once cancelled, the policy status reverts to "Normal," and you must pay manually via the portal or cash counter to avoid lapse.

115. What happens if ECS/NACH fails repeatedly?

Consequences of Payment Failure

When an automated debit (NACH) fails due to "Insufficient Funds" or "Account Closed," it triggers a sequence of financial and contractual penalties that can jeopardize your risk cover.

Financial Impact: Your bank will charge you a Penalty (ECS Return Charges), which can range from ₹250 to ₹500 per failure. LIC’s ANANDA system will mark the premium as "Unpaid." If the failure is not corrected within the grace period, the policy will Lapse. Online/Offline Recovery: After a failure, you must pay the premium Manually via the portal or the branch cash counter. The 3-Strike Rule: If the NACH fails for 3 consecutive months, LIC may Auto-Suspend the Mandate. To restart it, you must visit the branch, pay all arrears with interest, and submit a Fresh Mandate Form. Repeated failures also negatively impact your credit-like internal score at LIC, making future revivals or loan requests subject to stricter scrutiny.

116. Can premium be paid in advance for multiple years?

You cannot "Withdraw" the advance money once deposited; it can only be used for premiums.

117. Is there any rebate for yearly or half-yearly payment modes?

The Mode Rebate Advantage

LIC rewards policyholders who pay premiums less frequently because it reduces the "Transaction Cost" and "Administrative Load" for the corporation. These rewards are called Mode Rebates.

Calculation (System Logic): 1. Yearly Mode: You get a **2% Rebate** on the tabular premium. 2. Half-Yearly Mode: You get a **1% Rebate**. 3. Quarterly & Monthly: No rebate is offered. In fact, for Monthly (NACH) modes in some old plans, there was a small "Loading" or extra charge to cover the cost of 12 monthly debits. Online/Offline Check: You can see these rebates clearly in the Premium Illustration provided during the proposal. On the LIC Portal, you will notice that the "Yearly" premium is not just 12 times the "Monthly" premium—it is significantly lower. Practical Tip: Switching from Monthly to Yearly can save you thousands over a 20-year term. The ANANDA system applies these rebates automatically based on the mode recorded in the master file.

118. Can payment mode affect bonus or maturity value?

Mode vs. Policy Yield

There is a common myth that Yearly policies get more bonuses than Monthly ones. Legally and actuarially, the answer is **No**. The bonus is always calculated as a percentage of the **Basic Sum Assured**, not the premium paid.

The Actuarial Reality (ANANDA): Whether you pay ₹1 Lakh once a year or ₹8,500 every month, your Sum Assured (e.g., ₹20 Lakhs) remains the same. Therefore, the **Simple Reversionary Bonus** added to both policies every August will be identical. The Indirect Advantage: While the *Bonus* doesn't change, the **Maturity Yield (ROI)** is higher for Yearly modes. Because you paid less total premium (due to the 2% rebate) to get the same maturity amount, your internal rate of return is better. Online Tracking: You can verify this by checking the "Accumulated Bonus" on the portal for two identical policies with different modes—the figures will be the same. The "Mode" is an administrative frequency, not a risk-rating factor.

119. How does LIC handle excess or duplicate premium payments?

Handling Overpayments

Adjustment (Preferred): LIC will keep the extra money in a "Suspense Account" and automatically apply it to your **Next Premium Due**. You will see this as a "Credit Balance" on your portal login. 2. Refund (Offline Request): If you want the money back (e.g., if it was a large yearly premium), you must visit the branch and submit a "Refund Application." You must provide the transaction ID and your bank details. Caution: Do not file a "Chargeback" with your credit card company, as this can lead to the Blacklisting of your policy in the LIC payment gateway. Always resolve overpayments through the branch.

131. Which policy alterations are allowed only once in a policy lifetime?

132. Why are some endorsements chargeable while others are free?

Free Services (Online/Offline): Updating your contact details (Address, Mobile, Email) and Nomination is 100% free. LIC encourages these updates because they improve communication and reduce claim disputes. These are considered **Routine Maintenance** in the ANANDA system. Chargeable Services (Offline): Any change that requires a "Manual Underwriting Review" or a "Physical Document Re-issuance" is chargeable. For example, getting a **Duplicate Bond** attracts a fee based on the Sum Assured. Changing the payment mode (Monthly to Yearly) or altering the policy term requires a recalculation of the actuarial reserve, for which LIC charges a nominal "Alteration Fee" (usually ₹50 to ₹200). Transparency: You can pay these fees **Online** via the "Miscellaneous Payments" link on the portal or at the branch cash counter. The fee covers the administrative cost of the manual audit and the printing of the endorsement slip.

133. Can sum assured be increased after policy issuance?

The Immutable Sum Assured

A life insurance policy is an "Unilateral Contract" where the risk is assessed and the price (premium) is locked at the moment of entry. Because of this, LIC **Generally prohibits increasing the Sum Assured** of an existing policy.

The Reason (Actuarial): If SA increases were allowed mid-term, a person diagnosed with a terminal illness would immediately increase their cover to ₹1 Crore. This would lead to the collapse of the Life Fund due to "Anti-Selection." The Solution (Online/Offline): If you need more insurance, the only official way is to **Buy a New Policy**. This will require a new proposal form, fresh medical tests (if needed), and the premium will be based on your *current* age. Online Visibility: You can see your SA on the portal, but it is a "Read-Only" field. While you can *reduce* the SA (Offline only) if you cannot afford premiums, the ANANDA system will never allow an upward revision. This rule ensures that the risk pool remains balanced and the premiums stay low for all healthy policyholders.

134. Can policy term or PPT be changed later?

Alteration of Time Horizons

The **Policy Term** (the duration of cover) and the **PPT** (Premium Paying Term) are the core pillars of the policy's math. Altering these is possible, but it is a complex process that must be handled **Offline** at the servicing branch.

PPT Changes: You generally **Cannot Decrease the PPT** (e.g., changing a 20-pay policy to a 10-pay policy). This is because the earlier years' premiums would have been much higher, and the ANANDA system cannot retrospectively collect that extra money. Term Changes: You **Can increase the Policy Term** in some plans, provided the policy is still in its early years. This is called an "Extension of Term." Requirements: This requires a fresh health declaration (DGH) and sometimes medical tests, as LIC is now covering you for a longer duration. You may also have to pay a small "Alteration Fee." **Online Status:** You can check your "Term and PPT" on the portal dashboard, but any request for change must be submitted physically. The underwriter will review the request and, if approved, an "Endorsement Slip" will be pasted on your bond.

135. What is the role of underwriting in post-issuance alterations?

Underwriting ensures that alterations don't become a loophole for high-risk individuals to change their contract terms after their health has declined. This rigorous process maintains the "Equitable Risk" balance of the entire corporation.

137. What is the difference between endorsement and correction?

Correction vs. Endorsement

In LIC terminology, "Correction" and "Endorsement" are two different ways of updating the **ANANDA** master record. One fixes the past, while the other changes the future.

Correction (Offline/Free): This is used to fix a **Data Entry Error**. If the branch typed "Smit" instead of "Smith," or entered the wrong gender, this is a correction. It is usually free, but requires proof (like a Passport or School Certificate) to show what the "Original Truth" was. Endorsement (Offline/Online/Paid): This is a **Contractual Amendment**. It is used when you want to *change* something that was previously correct. For example, changing your nominee (Online/Free) or changing your payment mode (Offline/Paid). The Bond: For corrections, LIC may sometimes issue a "Corrected Bond." For endorsements, they usually provide an **Endorsement Slip** (a piece of paper) that you must stick onto the back of your original bond. Both are legally valid, but an endorsement always carries a "Effective Date" from when the new rule applies.

138. How are alterations reflected in bond and portal records?

142. How does LIC differentiate early claim vs non-early claim?

The Three-Year Risk Boundary

The distinction between "Early" and "Non-Early" claims is a legal framework derived from **Section 45 of the Insurance Act**. It determines the level of scrutiny LIC applies before releasing the Life Fund's money.

Early Claim (System Flag): If death occurs within **3 years** of the policy start or the last revival, the ANANDA system automatically flags it as "Early." LIC is legally permitted to investigate the truthfulness of the original health declarations. Offline Process: A "Claim Investigator" will visit the deceased's home, hospital, and employer to check if any pre-existing illness was hidden. Non-Early Claim: If death happens after 3 years, the policy becomes "Indisputable." LIC generally cannot reject these claims except in cases of extreme, proven fraud. Online Impact: You will see this difference in the portal status—Early claims will show "Pending Investigation," while Non-Early claims move directly to "Discharge Voucher Sent." This rule ensures that honest, long-term policyholders are not penalized by the costs of fraudulent "instant" claims by high-risk individuals.
